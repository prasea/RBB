### Overview of Financial Institutions in Nepal

The Nepalese financial system is broadly categorized into two major pillars:

1. **Banking Institutions (BFIs):** Regulated and supervised directly by **Nepal Rastra Bank (NRB)** under the **Bank and Financial Institutions Act (BAFIA), 2073**.
2. **Non-Bank Financial Institutions (NBFIs):** Contractual savings institutions, social security funds, and specialized agencies regulated by dedicated acts and authorities (e.g., Nepal Insurance Authority, Ministry of Finance, SSF Board).

---

### 1.1 Banking Institutions (BFIs)

NRB classifies licensed banks and financial institutions into four main categories based on minimum capital requirements and scope of operations (Classes 'Class A', 'Class B', 'Class C', and 'Class D'), along with a specialized **Infrastructure Development Bank**.

#### BFI Institutional Landscape (Current Status Summary)

* **Class 'A' Commercial Banks:** 20 institutions | Min. Capital: NPR 8 Billion
* **Class 'B' Development Banks:** 17 institutions | Min. Capital: NPR 2.5 Billion (National level)
* **Class 'C' Finance Companies:** 16 institutions | Min. Capital: NPR 800 Million (National level)
* **Class 'D' Microfinance Institutions (MFIs):** 51 institutions | Min. Capital: NPR 100 Million (National level)
* **Infrastructure Development Bank (NIFRA):** 1 institution | Min. Capital: NPR 20 Billion

---

#### 1.1.1 Commercial Banks (Class 'A')

* **Definition & Framework:** Commercial banks are full-service monetary institutions authorized to conduct all major commercial banking functions under BAFIA, 2073.
* **Minimum Paid-Up Capital:** NPR 8 Billion.
* **Core Roles & Functions:**
* Deposit Mobilization (Current, Savings, Fixed, Call deposits) and Credit Creation.
* Trade Financing: Issuance of Letters of Credit (LC), Bank Guarantees, Remittance services, Foreign Exchange trading.
* Payment & Settlement Systems: Operating clearing houses, electronic funds transfers, RTGS, mobile/internet banking.
* Direct priority-sector lending (minimum 15% to Agriculture, 10% to Energy/Hydropower, 15% to MSMEs((Micro, Small, and Medium Enterprises) ) as per NRB Directives).


* **Contribution to Nepal's Economy:**
* **Asset & Deposit Dominance:** Commercial banks account for over **83%–87%** of total banking assets and deposits in Nepal.
* **Financial Inclusion:** Branches operate in 752 out of 753 local levels across Nepal.
* Major drivers of corporate financing, infrastructure funding, and digital economy transformation.


* **Current Status:** 20 commercial banks are currently operational (including public sector banks like Rastriya Banijya Bank, Nepal Bank, and Agricultural Development Bank). The sector has experienced significant consolidation via M&A policies encouraged by NRB.

---

#### 1.1.2 Development Banks (Class 'B')

* **Definition & Framework:** Financial institutions established primarily to extend long-term credit and development financing to local industries, agriculture, and regional projects.
* **Minimum Paid-Up Capital:**
* National Level: NPR 2.5 Billion
* 1 to 3 Districts (Regional): NPR 500 Million


* **Core Roles & Functions:**
* Term-loans for small-to-medium project financing, cottage industries, agriculture, and real estate.
* Mobilizing local savings and providing basic retail banking services (savings, fixed deposits, localized loans).
* Acting as a financial bridge between rural microfinance and large commercial banks.


* **Contribution & Status:** 17 development banks are currently operating. They hold approximately **7%–8%** of total banking system assets, serving as key drivers of regional entrepreneurship and semi-urban economic development.

---

#### 1.1.3 Finance Companies (Class 'C')

* **Definition & Framework:** Non-bank-like financial institutions specializing in consumer credit, hire-purchase leasing, housing loans, and specialized asset financing.
* **Minimum Paid-Up Capital:**
* National Level: NPR 800 Million
* 1 to 3 Districts: NPR 300 Million


* **Core Roles & Functions:**
* Provision of Hire-Purchase (auto/vehicle loans), Term Loans, Personal Loans, and Equipment Leasing.
* Issuance of Fixed Deposits and Time Deposits.
* Specialized investment advisory and capital market services (historical role before merchant banking split).


* **Contribution & Status:** 16 finance companies are operational. They hold approximately **1.8%–2.0%** of total system assets, catering to niche retail, vehicle financing, and consumer loan markets.

---

#### 1.1.4 Microfinance Financial Institutions - MFIs (Class 'D')

* **Definition & Framework:** Special-class financial institutions formed to provide collateral-free microcredit, micro-savings, and financial literacy to underprivileged, rural, and low-income populations.
* **Minimum Paid-Up Capital:**
* National Level: NPR 100 Million


* **Core Roles & Functions:**
* Group-guarantee micro-lending model (Grameen Bank pattern).
* Promoting women empowerment, rural self-employment, and income-generating micro-enterprises.
* Collecting micro-deposits from group members.


* **Contribution & Status:** 51 MFIs operate across rural Nepal. They play an essential role in grassroots poverty alleviation and financial inclusion, though recent challenges include multi-banking debt stress and strict regulatory interest rate caps (15% maximum lending rate set by NRB).

---

#### 1.1.5 Infrastructure Development Bank (NIFRA)

* **Definition & Framework:** Specialized financial institution established under BAFIA, 2073, explicitly mandated to invest in medium- and long-term mega-infrastructure projects.
* **Minimum Paid-Up Capital:** NPR 20 Billion (Nepal Infrastructure Bank - NIFRA is the sole institution).
* **Core Roles & Functions:**
* Financing major infrastructure projects (Hydropower, Roads, Transmission lines, Airports, Mass Transit).
* Issuing long-term infrastructure bonds (in local and foreign currencies).
* Equity participation, mezzanine financing, and syndicate loan structuring for mega-projects.


* **Contribution & Status:** Fills the long-term project financing gap that standard commercial banks struggle to cover due to asset-liability mismatch (ALM) constraints.

---

### 1.2 Non-Bank Financial Institutions (NBFIs)

NBFIs play a major role in contractual savings, long-term capital formation, institutional investment, and social security.

#### 1.2.1 Insurance Companies

* **Regulatory Body:** **Nepal Insurance Authority (Nepal Bima Pradhikaran)** governed by the **Insurance Act, 2079**.
* **Structure:** Separated into **Life Insurance Companies** and **Non-Life Insurance (General) Companies**, along with **Reinsurance Companies** (e.g., Nepal Re, Himalayan Re).
* **Minimum Paid-Up Capital:**
* Life Insurance: NPR 5 Billion
* Non-Life Insurance: NPR 2.5 Billion


* **Core Roles & Contribution:**
* Risk transfers and financial protection against life losses, health emergencies, property damage, and commercial liabilities.
* Long-term capital accumulation through insurance premiums, which are invested in government bonds, bank deposits, and capital markets.
* Expanding micro-insurance and agricultural crop/livestock insurance in rural Nepal.



---

#### 1.2.2 Karmachari Sanchaya Kosh (Employees' Provident Fund - EPF)

* **Governing Legislation:** **Employees' Provident Fund Act, 2019 (1962)**.
* **Nature & Mandate:** Statutory autonomous body managing statutory provident fund contributions of civil servants, public sector employees, military, police, and participating private sector staff.
* **Core Functions:**
* Collection of mandatory provident fund contributions (10% employee contribution + 10% employer matching).
* Administration of social security schemes: Group Accidental Insurance, Funeral Support, Maternity Care, and Comprehensive Health Insurance.
* **Capital Deployment:** One of Nepal's largest institutional investors, funding national priority megaprojects (e.g., Upper Tamakoshi Hydropower Project), government securities, and housing/specialized loans for contributors.



---

#### 1.2.3 Nagarik Lagani Kosh (Citizen Investment Trust - CIT)

* **Governing Legislation:** **Citizen Investment Trust Act, 2047 (1990)**.
* **Nature & Mandate:** Statutory institutional body functioning under the Ministry of Finance to promote voluntary and mandatory savings among civil servants and the general public.
* **Core Schemes Managed:**
* Employee Savings Growth Investment Scheme.
* Investor Account Scheme & Civil Pension Scheme.
* Mutual funds, Unit schemes, and Gratuity Fund Management.


* **Core Roles & Contribution:**
* Mobilizes voluntary domestic savings into productive investments.
* Acts as a major market maker and institutional investor in Nepal's equity and primary share issuance markets (IPO underwriting and rights shares).



---

#### 1.2.4 Samajik Surakshya Kosh (Social Security Fund - SSF)

* **Governing Legislation:** **Contribution-based Social Security Act, 2074 (2017)**.
* **Nature & Mandate:** Formal social protection fund designed to establish universal social security coverage for formal and informal sector workers across Nepal.
* **Contribution Structure:**
* Total Contribution: **31% of Basic Salary** (Employer: 20% | Employee: 11%).


* **Four Core Social Security Schemes:**
1. Medical Care, Health, and Maternity Scheme (1%)
2. Accident and Disability Scheme (1.4%)
3. Dependent Family Protection Scheme (0.27%)
4. Old Age Protection Scheme / Pension Scheme (28.33%)


* **Strategic Role:** Ensures workplace security, reduces income vulnerability, minimizes social inequality, and creates a large fund pool for long-term national economic development.

### Practice Exam Questions (Level 5 Standard)

#### Section A: Subjective Questions (5 & 10 Marks)

##### **Question 1 (5 Marks):**

> **"Differentiate between Class 'A' Commercial Banks and Class 'D' Microfinance Financial Institutions in terms of capital, target coverage, and core operational objectives."**

**Sample Answer:**

**Introduction:** Both Class 'A' Commercial Banks and Class 'D' Microfinance Financial Institutions are licensed and regulated by Nepal Rastra Bank under BAFIA, 2073. However, they serve distinctly different structural roles in Nepal's economic ecosystem.

| Feature | Class 'A' Commercial Banks | Class 'D' Microfinance Institutions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Minimum Capital** | **NPR 8 Billion** | **NPR 100 Million** (National Level) |
| **Primary Target Market** | Corporate entities, MSMEs, trade finance, and urban/semi-urban retail clients. | Rural poor, low-income groups, landless individuals, and women entrepreneurs. |
| **Lending Model** | Collateral-based lending, corporate financing, foreign trade (LC/Guarantees). | Group-guarantee microcredit without traditional physical collateral. |
| **Deposit Collection** | General public (Current, Savings, Fixed, Call deposits) without restriction. | Primary focus on group member savings and micro-deposits. |
| **Primary Objective** | Profit maximization, trade facilitation, liquidity management, and broad monetary transmission. | Poverty reduction, financial inclusion, rural self-employment, and social empowerment. |

**Conclusion:** While Commercial Banks form the backbone of national macroeconomic stability and trade, Microfinance Institutions act as vital instruments for micro-level financial inclusion and rural poverty alleviation.

---

##### **Question 2 (10 Marks):**

> **"Discuss the role and structural contribution of Non-Bank Financial Institutions (NBFIs) like EPF (KSK), CIT (NLK), and SSF in the economic development of Nepal. What challenges do they face in capital deployment?"**

**Sample Answer:**

**1. Introduction:**
Non-Bank Financial Institutions (NBFIs) in Nepal, primarily statutory contractual savings organizations like **Karmachari Sanchaya Kosh (EPF)**, **Nagarik Lagani Kosh (CIT)**, and **Samajik Surakshya Kosh (SSF)**, complement the formal banking sector. They mobilize long-term savings, offer social protection, and channel patient capital into physical and social infrastructure.

**2. Key Structural Contributions:**

* **Long-Term Capital Formation:** Unlike commercial bank deposits (which are predominantly short-to-medium term), EPF, CIT, and SSF handle multi-decade retirement funds. This enables non-inflationary, long-term national investment.
* **Infrastructure Mega-Project Financing:** EPF and CIT serve as primary direct lenders to national strategic projects (e.g., EPF's sole financing participation in the 456 MW Upper Tamakoshi Hydropower Project, national airlines fleet financing, and transmission corridors).
* **Capital Market Stability:** CIT acts as a major institutional investor, underwriter, and market maker in the Nepalese capital market, helping to stabilize stock market fluctuations.
* **Social Protection & Human Capital Development:** SSF guarantees income security against medical emergencies, workplace accidents, disability, and old-age retirement, reducing economic vulnerability for workers.

**3. Major Challenges in Capital Deployment:**

* **Limited Investment Avenues:** Heavy reliance on fixed deposits in commercial banks due to limited options in debt capital markets and private equity.
* **Asset-Liability Mismatch & Yield Pressures:** Declining interest rate environments reduce fund returns, making it challenging to offer competitive yields to contributors.
* **Regulatory Coordination Gaps:** Dual governance challenges where NBFIs operate under specialized parent acts while interacting with NRB policies and ministry directives.
* **Lending & Project Risk Management:** Infrastructure projects in Nepal frequently experience time and cost overruns, exposing long-term fund liquidity to execution risks.

**4. Conclusion:**
To maximize the macroeconomic impact of NBFIs, Nepal must expand structural debt instruments, encourage corporate bond markets, and enable safe international/domestic diversification under robust risk-management frameworks.

### Question 1 (Short Answer — 5 Marks)
**Discuss the role and operational distinction between Class 'A' Commercial Banks and Class 'D' Microfinance Financial Institutions in Nepal.**
#### Answer
Class 'A' Commercial Banks and Class 'D' Microfinance Financial Institutions (MFIs) represent the top and bottom tiers of Nepal’s banking hierarchy under BAFIA, 2073, serving distinct market segments:
1. **Paid-up Capital & Scale:**
* **Class 'A':** Requires a minimum paid-up capital of NPR 8 Billion, operating nationally with large scale retail and corporate branches.

2. **Target Client & Lending Mechanism:**
* **Class 'A':** Target corporate bodies, high-net-worth individuals, and general retail clients. Loans are extended based on primary/collateral security.
* **Class 'D':** Target low-income individuals, micro-entrepreneurs, and marginalized women. Loans are primarily provided through social collateral (Group Guarantee Mechanism) without physical collateral.
3. **Scope of Services:**
* **Class 'A':** Offers complete financial services including International Trade Finance (LCs, Guarantees), foreign exchange dealing, treasury management, and complex payment processing.
* **Class 'D':** Restricted to collateral-free microcredit, group savings collection, and basic micro-insurance services.

### Unit 2: Key Basic Banking Terminology

In Nepalese banking, regulatory definitions and monetary tools are strictly enforced by **Nepal Rastra Bank (NRB)** through its **Unified Directives**, **Monetary Policy**, and regulatory frameworks like **Basel III**. Understanding these concepts provides the operational context necessary for implementing secure core banking systems, risk mitigation software, and digital financial workflows.

---

### 2.1 Capital Fund

* **Definition:** The safety cushion maintained by Banks and Financial Institutions (BFIs) to absorb financial losses (credit, market, and operational risks) and protect depositors' funds.
* **Regulatory Framework:** Governed by **NRB Capital Adequacy Framework (based on Basel III guidelines)**.
* **Core Components:**

$$\text{Total Capital Fund} = \text{Tier 1 Capital (Core Capital)} + \text{Tier 2 Capital (Supplementary Capital)}$$

1. **Tier 1 Capital (Core Capital):** High-quality capital capable of absorbing losses on a going-concern basis.
* *Components:* Paid-up equity capital, Share Premium, Statutory General Reserve, Retained Earnings, Capital Redemption Reserve.
* *Regulatory Floor:* Commercial Banks (Class 'A') must maintain a minimum **Common Equity Tier 1 (CET1) of 4.5%** and a minimum **Tier 1 Core Capital Ratio of 6.0%** of total Risk-Weighted Assets (RWA).


2. **Tier 2 Capital (Supplementary Capital):** Capital that absorbs losses on a gone-concern basis during liquidation.
* *Components:* Subordinated term debt/debentures, General Loan Loss Provisioning (up to 1.25% of RWA), Assets Revaluation Reserves (up to 2%), Investment Adjustment Reserve.



* **Minimum Capital Adequacy Ratio (CAR):** Class 'A' commercial banks must maintain a minimum **Total Capital Fund Ratio of 11.0%** of total Risk-Weighted Exposures (inclusive of the 2.5% Capital Conservation Buffer).

---

### 2.2 Loan Loss Provisioning (LLP)

* **Definition:** The mandatory setting aside of profits by BFIs to cover potential default losses on loans and advances based on their default risk profile and overdue period.
* **Regulatory Framework:** Governed by **NRB Unified Directive No. 2** (integrated with **NFRS 9 Expected Credit Loss Framework**).
* **Loan Classification & Provisioning Rates:**

```
                                  ┌───────────────────────────────────────────────┐
                                  │      Total Loans and Advances Portfolio       │
                                  └───────────────────────┬───────────────────────┘
                                                          │
                        ┌─────────────────────────────────┴─────────────────────────────────┐
                        │                                                                   │
           ┌────────────┴─────────────┐                                        ┌────────────┴─────────────┐
           │     Performing Loans     │                                        │  Non-Performing Loans    │
           │  (General Provisioning)  │                                        │  (Specific Provisioning) │
           └────────────┬─────────────┘                                        └────────────┬─────────────┘
                        │                                                                   │
       ┌────────────────┴────────────────┐                              ┌───────────────────┼───────────────────┐
       │                                 │                              │                   │                   │
┌──────┴──────┐                   ┌──────┴──────┐                ┌──────┴──────┐     ┌──────┴──────┐     ┌──────┴──────┐
│    Pass     │                   │  Watchlist  │                │ Substandard │     │  Doubtful   │     │    Loss     │
│  (0 - 1 mo) │                   │  (1 - 3 mo) │                │  (3 - 6 mo) │     │ (6 - 12 mo) │     │ (> 12 mo)   │
│   Rate: 1%  │                   │   Rate: 5%  │                │  Rate: 25%  │     │  Rate: 50%  │     │  Rate: 100% │
└─────────────┘                   └─────────────┘                └─────────────┘     └─────────────┘     └─────────────┘

```

| Classification Status | Overdue Period | Loan Category | Provision Rate | Provision Type |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Pass** | Overdue up to 1 Month (0–30 Days) | Performing | **1.14% / 1%** | General Provision |
| **Watchlist** | Overdue from 1 to 3 Months (31–90 Days) | Performing | **5%** | General Provision |
| **Substandard** | Overdue from 3 to 6 Months (91–180 Days) | Non-Performing (NPL) | **25%** | Specific Provision |
| **Doubtful** | Overdue from 6 Months to 1 Year (181–365 Days) | Non-Performing (NPL) | **50%** | Specific Provision |
| **Loss (Bad Loan)** | Overdue for more than 1 Year (> 365 Days) | Non-Performing (NPL) | **100%** | Specific Provision |

* *Note:* Loans extended against security of Fixed Deposit receipts, NRB bonds, and Government of Nepal securities require **0% loan loss provision**.

---

### 2.3 Credit-Deposit (CD) Ratio

* **Definition:** A key liquidity management metric that measures the proportion of mobilized deposits that a bank has deployed as loans and credit extensions.
* **Formula:**

$$\text{CD Ratio} = \left( \frac{\text{Total Credit / Loans Extended}}{\text{Total Deposit Mobilized} + \text{Core Capital}} \right) \times 100\%$$

* **NRB Mandate:** Commercial banks in Nepal are required to maintain a **maximum CD Ratio of 90%**.
* **Objective:** Prevents over-lending, ensures liquidity buffers for unexpected deposit withdrawals, and replaces the historical Credit-to-Core-Deposit (CCD) ratio to align with international prudential standards.

---

### 2.4 Cash Reserve Ratio (CRR)

* **Definition:** The minimum percentage of a bank's total local currency deposits that must be maintained as mandatory cash balances in an account with Nepal Rastra Bank.
* **Mandated Rate:** **4.0%** for Commercial Banks (Class 'A'), Development Banks (Class 'B'), and Finance Companies (Class 'C').
* **Key Features:**
* Non-interest bearing balance (NRB pays 0% interest on mandatory CRR reserves).
* Serves as a primary monetary policy control tool to adjust liquidity in the financial system.
* *Penalty:* Non-compliance attracts monetary fines calculated based on the prevailing **Bank Rate**.

### 2.5 Refinancing, Rescheduling, and Restructuring

These three tools are used to support borrowers and manage credit portfolio stress:

```

                                                 │

  ┌──────┴──────┐                         ┌──────┴──────┐                         ┌──────┴──────┐

  │ (Liquidity) │                         │  (Timeline) │                         │  (Terms)    │

```

1. **Refinancing (पुनर्कर्जा):**
* *Mechanism:* A liquidity support facility where NRB provides concessional loans to BFIs against their existing loan portfolios, enabling them to extend lower-cost credit to targeted priority sectors, small enterprises, or export business operations.


2. **Rescheduling (पुनःतालिकीकरण):**
* *Mechanism:* Extending or altering the loan repayment schedule (tenure/time period) without changing the core contractual loan terms or loan amount, applied when a cash-flow delay occurs due to temporary external circumstances.


3. **Restructuring (पुनःसंरचना):**
* *Mechanism:* Modifying primary contractual loan terms (e.g., reducing interest rates, waiving penalty charges, converting portion of debt to equity, or changing installment structures) for distressed borrowers with viable business potential.
* *Regulatory Rule:* Restructured or rescheduled loans must maintain a **minimum of 12.5% to 25% loan loss provision** depending on their pre-restructuring classification.

### 2.6 Base Rate

* **Definition:** The standard benchmark cost of funds calculated by BFIs using a uniform formula prescribed by NRB, below which no bank is permitted to extend commercial loans (except for special policy/concessional categories).
* **Base Rate Components:**

$$\text{Base Rate} = \text{Cost of Funds} + \text{Cost of Mandatory Cash Reserves (CRR)} + \text{Statutory Liquidity Ratio (SLR) Cost} + \text{Operating Costs}$$

* **Lending Rate Mechanics:**

$$\text{Lending Rate} = \text{Base Rate} + \text{Risk Premium Percentage}$$

* **Significance:** Ensures price transparency, protects borrowers from arbitrary interest rate hikes, and provides a clear mechanism for market-driven lending rates.

### 2.7 Credit Information and Blacklisting

* **Credit Information Bureau (CIB - कर्जा सूचना केन्द्र):** An independent entity in Nepal that collects, maintains, and shares credit profiles and default histories of borrowers across all licensed financial institutions.
* **Blacklisting Criteria (NRB Unified Directive No. 12):**
* Failure to clear principal or interest overdue for **more than 90 days** despite formal notices.
* Bouncing of checks due to insufficient balance (**Dishonor of Cheque**) after formal 30-day legal notice.
* Misutilization or diversion of loan funds from the approved business purpose.
* Submission of fraudulent/fake collateral documents or audited balance sheets.
* Becoming insolvent or absconding.


* **Consequences of Blacklisting:**
* Complete restriction from taking new loans or operating current/checking bank accounts in any BFI in Nepal.
* Passport impoundment and prohibition from holding public or board-level positions in corporate/financial entities.

## 2. Comprehensive Explanatory Notes

### 2.1 Capital Fund (Capital Adequacy Ratio)

The Capital Fund represents the cushion a bank maintains to absorb potential losses, protecting depositors and ensuring stability.

* **Tier 1 Capital (Core Capital):** High-quality, permanent capital. Includes Paid-up Capital, Share Premium, Retained Earnings, General Reserve, and Capital Redemption Reserve.
* **Tier 2 Capital (Supplementary Capital):** Secondary capital including Subordinated Debt, Hybrid Capital Instruments, General Loan Loss Provisions, and Exchange Equalization Reserve.
* **Regulatory Standard:** Under NRB's Capital Adequacy Framework (Basel II/III), commercial banks in Nepal must maintain a minimum Total Capital Adequacy Ratio (CAR) of **11%** (including a Capital Conservation Buffer) and a minimum Tier 1 Capital ratio of **8.5%**.

### 2.2 Loan Loss Provisioning

NRB Unified Directives require banks to classify assets into two broad categories and five sub-classes, creating specific loan loss provisions accordingly:

* **Definition:** The proportion of total credit/loans extended by a bank relative to its total deposit collection.

### 2.4 Cash Reserve Ratio (CRR)

* **Definition:** The mandatory percentage of total domestic deposits that financial institutions must keep as cash balances directly with Nepal Rastra Bank.
* **Current Requirement:** **4%** for Class 'A', 'B', and 'C' financial institutions (as mandated by current NRB Monetary Policy).
* **Purpose:** Serves as a primary monetary policy tool to control money supply, manage inflation, and ensure liquidity reserves.

### 2.5 Refinancing, Rescheduling, and Restructuring

These tools manage liquidity and loan recovery during borrower distress:

* **Refinancing (*Punarqarja*):** A facility provided by NRB where it offers low-cost liquidity to banks to extend subsidized credit to targeted sectors (e.g., export businesses, agriculture, or impacted SMEs).
* **Rescheduling:** Extending the repayment timeline or maturity period of a loan without changing its principal terms or interest rate.
* **Restructuring:** Modifying the terms of a loan agreement, such as scaling down interest rates, capitalizing interest, or converting debt into equity to assist a temporarily distressed borrower.

### 2.6 Base Rate

* **Definition:** The minimum reference interest rate calculated by banks based on cost of funds, cost of cash reserve ratio, statutory liquidity ratio, and cost of operation.
* **Principle:** NRB mandates that banks **cannot lend below their Base Rate**, ensuring fair competition and preventing unviable predatory lending.
* **Lending Rate Formula:** $\text{Lending Rate} = \text{Base Rate} + \text{Risk Premium}$.

* **Credit Information Bureau (CIB):** An independent agency in Nepal that collects, maintains, and distributes credit history data of individuals and corporate borrowers.
* **Blacklisting Criteria:** Borrowers are recommended for CIB blacklisting in cases of:

### Question 1 (Short Answer — 5 Marks)

**Differentiate between Loan Rescheduling and Loan Restructuring. Explain the loan provisioning rules applicable to rescheduled or restructured loans as per NRB directives.**

#### Answer

Loan Rescheduling and Loan Restructuring are credit recovery tools used to manage delinquent loans:

1. **Operational Difference:**
* **Rescheduling:** Refers strictly to extending the payment tenure or modifying repayment frequency/instalment dates without altering interest terms or principal conditions.
* **Restructuring:** Refers to changing original terms of agreement, such as lowering interest rates, waiving partial penalties, capitalizing accrued interest, or converting debt into equity.


2. **NRB Provisioning Rules:**
* Loans that are rescheduled or restructured must be categorized based on their underlying risk assessment.
* A minimum provision of **12.5%** must be maintained for performing loans that are rescheduled or restructured due to genuine borrower difficulty.
* If a Non-Performing Loan (Substandard, Doubtful, or Loss) is rescheduled/restructured, its existing loan loss provision **cannot be downgraded to a lower provision level immediately**. It must maintain its original non-performing provision (e.g., 25%, 50%, or 100%) for at least **6 months** of consistent, satisfactory repayment performance before reclassification into a performing status.

### Question 2 (Short Answer — 5 Marks)

**What is the Base Rate in banking? Explain how Base Rate protects both financial institutions and depositors in Nepal.**

#### Answer

**Base Rate** is the standardized minimum reference rate calculated by banks in Nepal according to the formula specified by Nepal Rastra Bank (NRB). It factors in the cost of funds, cost of maintaining mandatory reserves (CRR and SLR), operating costs, and return on assets.

**How Base Rate Protects Financial Institutions and Depositors:**

1. **Ensures Financial Viability (Prevents Predatory Lending):** By restricting banks from lending below their Base Rate, NRB prevents destructive price wars where institutions extend credit below cost to capture market share, preserving system stability.
2. **Transparent Pricing for Borrowers:** Lending rates are explicitly tied to the Base Rate ($\text{Base Rate} + \text{Premium}$). Any decrease in a bank's cost of funds reduces the Base Rate, automatically lowering borrowing costs for existing variable-rate borrowers.
3. **Protects Depositors' Interest:** Maintaining margin integrity through the Base Rate ensures banks remain profitable and solvent, safeguarding depositor funds from bank failure.
4. **Enhances Monetary Transmission:** Adjustments in NRB's monetary policy directly impact the cost of funds, which flows into the Base Rate and adjusts lending rates systematically across the market.

### Question 3 (Long Answer — 10 Marks)

**Define Non-Performing Loans (NPL). Detail the classification of assets and corresponding loan loss provisioning guidelines issued by Nepal Rastra Bank. Discuss the operational impact of rising NPLs on commercial banks like Rastriya Banijya Bank.**

#### Answer

##### 1. Introduction

A **Non-Performing Loan (NPL)** is a loan or advance where the borrower has failed to make scheduled payments of principal or interest for a period exceeding 90 days (3 months). NPLs represent asset quality degradation and are a primary indicator of credit risk in banking institutions.

##### 2. Asset Classification and Loan Loss Provisioning Guidelines

Nepal Rastra Bank (NRB) through its Unified Directives mandates a five-tier asset classification based on the duration of overdue payments, split into Performing and Non-Performing categories:

| Category | Loan Classification | Overdue Period | Mandatory Provision Rate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Performing** | **Pass** | Up to 1 Month | **1.14%** *(General Provision)* |
|  | **Watch List** | 1 Month to 3 Months | **5%** *(General Provision)* |
| **Non-Performing (NPL)** | **Substandard** | 3 Months to 6 Months | **25%** *(Specific Provision)* |
|  | **Doubtful** | 6 Months to 1 Year | **50%** *(Specific Provision)* |
|  | **Loss** | Over 1 Year (or insolvent/blacklisted) | **100%** *(Specific Provision)* |

*Note: Provisions for Pass and Watch List are General Loan Loss Provisions, while Substandard, Doubtful, and Loss are Specific Loan Loss Provisions.*

                                                                              └─ Loss: 100%

```

##### 3. Operational and Financial Impact of Rising NPLs on Banks (e.g., RBB)

When NPL levels rise in a bank, it causes cascading impacts across financial operations:

1. **Reduction in Net Profitability:** Specific provisions (25%, 50%, 100%) are deducted directly from operating profits as an expense, reducing net income and return on equity (ROE).
2. **Capital Adequacy Pressure:** Unrecovered principal write-offs and high provision requirements erode Tier 1 and Total Capital Adequacy Ratios (CAR), restricting the bank's capability to expand its loan portfolio.
3. **Liquidity Strain:** NPLs halt the flow of expected principal repayments and interest inflows, restricting liquidity and forcing banks to rely on costlier short-term deposits or NRB repo facilities.
4. **Increased Recovery and Legal Expenses:** High NPLs force banks to spend administrative resources on collateral auctioning, legal litigation, CIB blacklisting, and court cases (Debt Recovery Tribunal - DRT).
5. **Opportunity Cost & Stagnation:** Funds locked in non-performing assets produce no interest income, preventing allocation of capital toward high-yield, productive credit opportunities.

##### 4. Conclusion

Maintaining low NPL levels through rigorous pre-sanction credit appraisal, robust collateral valuation, continuous post-sanction monitoring, and early warning systems is vital for institutional health and financial system stability.

### 3.2 Bank and Financial Institution Act (BAFIA), 2073 (2017)
**BAFIA, 2073** governs the establishment, operation, management, and regulation of all licensed commercial banks and financial institutions in Nepal.

#### Key Statutory Provisions

* **Institutional Categorization (Section 31):** Categorizes licensed BFIs into **Class 'A' (Commercial Banks)**, **Class 'B' (Development Banks)**, **Class 'C' (Finance Companies)**, **Class 'D' (Microfinance Institutions)**, and **Infrastructure Development Bank** based on capital and business scope.
* **Incorporation & Licensing:** Requires prior approval and a formal license from NRB before commencing banking operations. Promoters must retain their capital shareholdings for a **mandatory lock-in period of 5 years**.
* **Corporate Governance & Board Composition:**
* Board size: Minimum **5** and Maximum **7** Directors.
* Mandates at least **1 Independent Director** with specialized experience in banking, finance, economics, or law.
* Disqualifies individuals with active defaults, criminal convictions, or concurrent executive/director roles in other financial institutions from serving on BFI boards.


* **Executive Leadership:** The Chief Executive Officer (CEO) is appointed by the Board for a **maximum term of 4 years**, renewable for one additional term (maximum 2 consecutive terms).
* **Capital & Reserve Requirements:** BFIs must maintain statutory reserves, including setting aside **20% of net annual profits** into a General Reserve Fund until it equals paid-up capital (and 10% annually thereafter).

#### Key Statutory Provisions

* **Definition of Money Laundering (Section 3):** Converting, transferring, acquiring, possessing, or concealing assets derived from predicate criminal activities (e.g., corruption, tax evasion, cybercrime, banking fraud, smuggling) to disguise their illegal origin.
* **Customer Due Diligence (CDD) & KYC (Section 7):** Reporting entities (Banks, Financial Institutions, Insurance, Real Estate) must verify customer identities, identify ultimate **Beneficial Owners (BOs)**, and conduct Enhanced Due Diligence (EDD) on **Politically Exposed Persons (PEPs)**.

### 3.5 Banking Offence and Punishment Act, 2064 (2008)

This Act defines specific criminal offenses against banks and financial institutions, establishing formal procedures for investigation, prosecution, and adjudication.

#### Key Offenses Defined (Sections 3 to 14)

* Opening accounts or demanding cash using false or fictitious documents.
* Unauthorized issuance, misuse, or alteration of electronic cards (ATMs, Credit/Debit cards, digital tokens).
* Obtaining or extending unauthorized loans using fake financial statements or overvalued collateral.
* Misusing or diverting project loan proceeds away from their approved statutory purpose.
* Dishonor of Cheque (Cheque Bounce) due to insufficient account balance.

#### Cheque Bounce Framework & Legal Reform

* **Cheque Dishonor Procedures:** When a cheque is returned due to insufficient funds, the bank issues a formal confirmation.
* **Investigative Authority:** Under recent legal updates, **Nepal Police** hold primary statutory authority to investigate cheque bounce complaints registered under the Banking Offence Act, replacing direct litigation procedures.
* **Penalties & Settlement:**
* Civil Liability: Full repayment of the cheque amount, plus 5% penalty and statutory interest.
* Criminal Penalty: Imprisonment terms scaled based on the suit amount (ranging up to **2 to 4 years** for amounts exceeding NPR 100 Million).
* **Deed of Compromise (मिलापत्र):** The law permits a formal execution of compromise if the offender pays the full amount owed to the cheque holder before judicial disposal, leading to the settlement of charges.

### 3.6 Negotiable Instruments Act, 2034 (1977)

This Act regulates fundamental commercial debt instruments used in Nepalese banking and financial markets.

#### Defined Negotiable Instruments

1. **Promissory Note (प्रतिज्ञापत्र):** An unconditional written promise made by one person to another, signed by the maker, engaging to pay on demand or at a fixed date a certain sum of money only to, or to the order of, a specified person or to the bearer.
2. **Bill of Exchange (विनिमय पत्र):** An unconditional written order signed by the drawer, directing a person (drawee) to pay a specified sum of money only to, or to the order of, a certain person or to the bearer.
3. **Cheque (चेक):** A specific type of Bill of Exchange drawn on a specified bank and payable strictly on demand.

#### Types of Cheque Endorsements & Crossings

* **Bearer Cheque:** Payable to whoever presents the cheque at the counter.
* **Order Cheque:** Payable to a specific person named on the instrument or their designated endorsee.
* **General Crossing (`& Co.` or two parallel lines):** Payment cannot be collected as cash at the bank counter; it must be cleared through a bank account.
* **Special Crossing:** Contains the name of a specific bank between two parallel lines, restricting collection strictly to that designated bank.

### 3.7 Electronic Transaction Act (ETA), 2063 (2006)
The **Electronic Transaction Act (ETA), 2063** serves as Nepal's primary Cyber Law, granting legal recognition to digital data, electronic records, and digital signatures while penalizing cybercrimes.

#### Key Statutory Provisions

* **Legal Recognition of Electronic Records (Section 3):** Any information, document, or record generated, sent, received, or stored in electronic form carries the same legal validity as traditional paper documents.
* **Digital Signatures (Chapter 3):**
* Legal validity of digital signatures generated using asymmetric cryptosystems (Public/Private key pairs).
* Establishes the office of the **Controller of Certifying Authorities (CCA)** to license and regulate **Certifying Authorities (CAs)** issuing digital signature certificates in Nepal.


* **Cybercrimes and Offenses Defined (Chapter 9):**
* **Unauthorized Access & Hacking (Section 52):** Intentional unauthorized access to computer systems, banking software, or digital networks.
* **Source Code Tampering (Section 48):** Altering, concealing, or destroying computer source code used for systems or applications.
* **Digital Forgery & Fraud:** Generating forged electronic records or unauthorized altered digital signatures.
* **Damage to Computer Systems:** Injecting malware, viruses, or launching Denial of Service (DoS/DDoS) attacks against digital infrastructure.


* **Adjudication Framework:** Establishes the **IT Tribunal (सूचना प्रविधि न्यायाधीकरण)** as an appellate judicial body to hear cases and disputes arising under cyber law.

## 2. Comprehensive Explanatory Notes

### 3.1 Nepal Rastra Bank Act, 2058

#### Preamble and Legal Status

Enacted to establish an autonomous central bank responsible for price stability, balance of payments stability, sustainable economic development, and financial sector stability. Under Section 3, NRB is a body corporate with perpetual succession, an independent seal, and the capacity to sue and be sued in its own name.

#### Objectives of NRB (Section 4)

1. Formulate and manage monetary and foreign exchange policies to maintain price and balance of payments stability.
2. Promote stability and enhance public confidence in the overall banking and financial system.
3. Develop a secure, healthy, and efficient payment system.
4. Regulate, inspect, supervise, and monitor the banking and financial system.

#### Key Functions, Duties, and Powers (Section 5)

* Exclusive right to issue banknotes and coins in Nepal.
* Formulate and implement monetary and foreign exchange policy.
* Determine the foreign exchange rate system and manage foreign exchange reserves.
* Issue operational licenses to BFIs, and inspect, supervise, or regulate their activities.
* Act as banker, advisor, and financial agent to the Government of Nepal.
* Act as the **Lender of Last Resort** to licensed BFIs.

#### Governance Structure

* **Board of Directors (7 Members):** Governed by a Board comprising the Governor (Chairperson), Secretary of Ministry of Finance, two Deputy Governors, and three non-executive Directors appointed from finance, banking, or economic experts.
* **Tenure:** Governor, Deputy Governors, and Directors serve a term of **5 years**.

### 3.2 Bank and Financial Institution Act (BAFIA), 2073

#### Objectives

Consolidates laws on the establishment, operation, management, and regulation of banks and financial institutions to build public confidence, safeguard depositor interest, and foster economic stability.

#### Incorporation and Public Issuance

* BFIs must incorporate as **Public Limited Companies** after obtaining prior approval from NRB.
* **Share Allocation (Section 9):** BFIs must allocate at least **30% of total issued capital** for subscription by the general public. A maximum of 0.5% may be reserved for employees.

#### Board of Directors (BOD) and Management (Chapter 4)

* **BOD Composition (Section 14):** Minimum 5 and maximum 7 directors. Must include at least **one Independent Director** appointed from an NRB-maintained expert list.
* **Disqualifications (Section 18):** Age below 21 years (or below 25 years for specific positions), declared insolvent, blacklisted, or currently serving as a director/employee in another BFI.
* **Chief Executive Officer (CEO) Qualification:** Bachelor's degree with minimum 5 years of executive-level experience in banking/finance, or Master's degree in a relevant field. Maximum term of **4 years per tenure**, eligible for re-appointment for one additional term.

#### Capital, Liquidity, and Risk Safeguards

* Requires maintaining minimum Capital Funds (CAR), Loan Loss Provisioning, General Reserve Fund (allocating 20% of net annual profit until double the paid-up capital), and Liquid Assets.
* **Recovery Rights:** If a borrower defaults or misuses credit, Section 57 empowers the BFI to auction or take ownership of collateralized asset security without prior court proceedings.

### 3.3 Payment and Settlement Act, 2075

#### Objectives

Enacted to provide legal grounding for non-cash payment instruments, electronic fund transfers, National Payment Systems, and the regulation of technology entities handling financial transactions.

#### Key Regulatory Framework

* **Licensing Mandate:** Any entity functioning as a **Payment System Operator (PSO)** (e.g., card networks, national switches) or **Payment Service Provider (PSP)** (e.g., digital wallets, QR aggregators) must obtain a formal license from NRB.
* **Finality of Settlement:** Grants legal finality to payment settlement instructions once processed through systems like Real Time Gross Settlement (RTGS) or Interbank Payment Systems (IPS).

2. **Bill of Exchange:** An unconditional order in writing, addressed by one person to another, signed by the person giving it, requiring the person to whom it is addressed to pay a sum certain in money.

#### Core Concepts

### Question 3 (Long Answer — 10 Marks)

**Analyze the key provisions of the Banking Offence and Punishment Act, 2064 and the Electronic Transaction Act, 2063 regarding IT-related banking frauds. How do these two acts complement each other in prosecuting cybercrimes in commercial banks?**

#### Answer

##### 1. Introduction

With the digitalization of banking (Mobile Banking, CBS, APIs, and ATMs), technology-based financial crimes present operational and legal risks. In Nepal, prosecutions for digital financial crimes rely on both the **Banking Offence and Punishment Act, 2064** and the **Electronic Transaction Act, 2063**.

##### 2. Key IT-Related Provisions in the Banking Offence & Punishment Act, 2064

The Banking Offence Act focuses on financial fraud, unauthorized fund extractions, and administrative abuses within financial institutions:

* **Unauthorized System Access for Financial Gain:** Prosecutes individuals who fraudulently access CBS or payment systems to credit accounts, alter ledgers, or execute unauthorized fund transfers.
* **Tampering with Banking Records:** Penalizes internal employees or external bad actors who falsify digital entries, statements, or accounting logs.
* **Card and ATM Frauds:** Penalizes cloning payment cards, skimming ATM credentials, or altering transaction switches.

##### 3. Key Provisions in the Electronic Transaction Act (ETA), 2063

ETA 2063 acts as the broader cyber-law framework, establishing the legal validity of digital evidence and penalizing technical computer breaches:

* **Section 44–58 (Computer Crimes):** Codifies unauthorized access to computer systems, data theft, insertion of malicious software/viruses, source code alteration, and digital identity theft.
* **Legal Validity of Electronic Evidence:** Establishes that digital logs, database entries, system backups, and network traffic traces are admissible as evidence in legal proceedings.
* **Certifying Authority Framework:** Provides statutory validity to digital signatures used in high-value electronic fund transfers.

##### 4. Complementary Relationship in Prosecuting Cybercrime

These statutes work together to address both the technology breach and the resulting financial crime:

```
                          ┌────────────────────────────────────────┐
                          │   Prosecuting Digital Banking Fraud    │
                          └───────────────────┬────────────────────┘
                                              │
        ┌─────────────────────────────────────┴─────────────────────────────────────┐
        │                                                                           │
        ▼                                                                           ▼
Electronic Transaction Act, 2063                                    Banking Offence & Punishment Act, 2064
├─ Defines the Cyber/IT Offence                                     ├─ Defines the Financial Crime

* **Technical Offence vs. Financial Crime:** ETA 2063 covers the technical breach (e.g., unauthorized database access or phishing), while the Banking Offence Act covers the resulting financial crime (e.g., unauthorized fund diversion or illegal ledger manipulation).
* **Evidence Validation:** ETA 2063 validates digital logs, database backups, and IP trails so they can be admitted as evidence in cases prosecuted under the Banking Offence Act.
* **Penal Severity and Asset Recovery:** Combining both acts allows law enforcement to seek criminal penalties for computer crimes alongside mandatory financial restitution to recover stolen bank funds.

Together, the Banking Offence Act, 2064 and Electronic Transaction Act, 2063 provide a complementary legal framework. ### 5.1 Companies Act, 2063 (2006)



The **Companies Act, 2063** provides the statutory framework for the incorporation, capital structure, corporate governance, financial reporting, and liquidation of companies in Nepal. While BAFIA, 2073 acts as a specialized law for financial institutions, the Companies Act governs baseline corporate operations.

#### Key Provisions

* **Incorporation & Corporate Status (Section 3 & 7):** Establishes Rastriya Banijya Bank as a **Public Limited Company (`RBB Ltd.`)** with limited liability, perpetual succession, a corporate seal, and the right to hold property and sue or be sued in its corporate name.
* **Capital Structure & Shares (Chapter 3):**
* **Authorized Capital:** Maximum equity capital the bank is legally permitted to issue.
* **Issued Capital:** Portion of authorized capital offered to shareholders/public.
* **Paid-Up Capital:** Actual capital remitted by shareholders (must meet NRB's NPR 8 Billion minimum threshold for commercial banks).


* **Annual General Meeting - AGM (Section 67):** Public limited companies must conduct an AGM within 6 months of the end of each fiscal year to approve audited financial statements, elect board directors, appoint external auditors, and declare dividends.
* **Board of Directors & Fiduciary Duties (Chapter 5):** Directors must exercise reasonable care, skill, and diligence. The Act mandates statutory committees, including an **Audit Committee** chaired by a non-executive director.
* **Financial Accounting & Audit (Chapter 8):** Financial accounts must be maintained according to **Nepal Financial Reporting Standards (NFRS)** and audited annually by an independent Chartered Accountant approved by the AGM/NRB.

### 5.3 Public Procurement Act, 2063 (2006)



The **Public Procurement Act, 2063** (and Public Procurement Rules, 2064) ensures transparency, equal opportunity, competitiveness, and accountability when public entities—including state-owned or public commercial banks like Rastriya Banijya Bank—procure goods, services, or construction works.

#### Key Procurement Methods (Section 13)

1. **International Open Bidding:** For high-value procurements requiring global competition.
2. **National Open Bidding:** Standard method for goods, works, and services available within Nepal above prescribed financial limits.
3. **Sealed Quotations (सिलबन्दी दरभाउपत्र):** For low-value off-the-shelf goods or minor works within prescribed statutory limits.
4. **Direct Procurement (सोझै खरिद):** Reserved for low-value purchases or single-source situations (e.g., emergency system maintenance, proprietary software license renewals).
5. **Community Participation & User Committees:** Used primarily in local development projects.

#### Key Procedural Rules

* **Bidding Documents & Technical Specifications:** Procurement documents must avoid proprietary bias and state clear, objective evaluation criteria.
* **Bid Security & Performance Security:**
* **Bid Bond (Bayada):** Typically **2% to 3%** of the estimated tender value to ensure bid validity.
* **Performance Guarantee:** Typically **5%** of the contract value deposited by the winning bidder upon signing.


* **Blacklisting of Contractors/Suppliers:** The **Public Procurement Monitoring Office (PPMO)** can blacklist defaulting suppliers or vendors for **1 to 3 years** based on fraud, performance default, or non-execution.

### 6.1 Electronic Cheque Clearing (NCHL-ECC)

**Electronic Cheque Clearing (ECC)** replaces paper-based cheque clearing with an image-based digital truncation system.

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                   Electronic Cheque Clearing (ECC)                     │
└───────────────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────────────┘
                                    │
  ┌─────────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────┐
  │                                 │                                 │
┌─┴───────────────────────┐ ┌───────┴─────────────────┐ ┌─────────────┴───────────┐
│ Presenting Bank Scans   │ │ Digital Transmission    │ │ Paying Bank Verifies    │
│ Paper Cheque (MICR +    │─┼─► via NCHL Secure Net  ─┼─► Signature & Image for │
│ High-Res Image)         │ │ (Digital Certificate)   │ │ Electronic Settlement   │
└─────────────────────────┘ └─────────────────────────┘ └─────────────────────────┘

```

* **Core Mechanism:** The physical paper cheque is presented at the beneficiary bank branch, digitized into high-resolution images, and parsed via **Magnetic Ink Character Recognition (MICR)** lines (containing cheque number, routing number, and account number).
* **Operational Flow:**
1. *Capture:* Presenting bank captures digital images (front and back) and MICR data.
2. *Transmission:* Encrypted images are transmitted through the secure NCHL-ECC clearing engine.
3. *Validation & Settlement:* The paying bank verifies account details, balances, and digital signatures, authorizing electronic debit and credit settlement through BFI settlement accounts at NRB.


* **Benefits:** Reduces clearing cycles from several days to same-day settlement, lowers paper handling and transportation costs, and mitigates loss or theft in transit.

### 6.4 Digital Payment Cards

### 6.6 Central Bank Digital Currency (CBDC)

**Central Bank Digital Currency (CBDC)** is a digital form of a country's sovereign fiat currency issued and backed directly by the central bank.

#### 1. Structural Comparison

* **Cash (Physical Fiat):** Direct liability of the Central Bank; anonymous, offline, universally accessible, non-digital.
* **Commercial Bank Money:** Electronic liability of private commercial banks; vulnerable to institution-specific liquidity risks.
* **CBDC:** Direct digital liability of the Central Bank; legally recognized fiat money accessible electronically without commercial bank balance-sheet risk.

#### 2. Architecture & Design Paradigms

* **Retail CBDC:** Designed for public use in day-to-day transactions by consumers and businesses.
* **Wholesale CBDC:** Restricted to financial institutions for interbank clearing, reserve management, and wholesale cross-border settlements.
* **Two-Tier Model:** The Central Bank issues and manages the underlying ledger for CBDC, while commercial banks and payment providers handle customer distribution, wallet interfaces, and KYC management.

#### 3. Strategic Context & Progress in Nepal

* **NRB Feasibility Studies:** NRB established a dedicated **CBDC Division** following initial exploratory studies to evaluate operational frameworks, legal adjustments, and infrastructure choices.
* **Key Policy Objectives:**
1. Reduce physical banknote printing, distribution, and destruction costs.
2. Broaden financial inclusion across remote geographic areas.
3. Ensure resilience in payment systems against private cryptocurrency proliferation and cross-border digital capital flight.

## 2. Comprehensive Explanatory Notes
### 6.1 Electronic Cheque Clearing (NCHL-ECC)
#### Overview and Evolution
Before electronic clearing, interbank cheque settlement required physical paper delivery to clearing houses, taking 2 to 3 days. The **NCHL-ECC System** (operated by Nepal Clearing House Limited) processes cheque clearing electronically using High-Resolution Cheque Images and Magnetic Ink Character Recognition (MICR) or Optical Character Recognition (OCR) data.
```
┌─────────────────┐     MICR / Image Data      ┌─────────────────┐
│ Presenting Bank │ ─────────────────────────► │   NCHL-ECC      │
│  (Deposit Bank) │                            │ Central Clearing│
└─────────────────┘                            └────────┬────────┘
        ▲                                               │
        │ Net Settlement                                │ Image Verification
        ▼                                               ▼
┌─────────────────┐                            ┌─────────────────┐
│   Nepal Rastra  │ ◄───────────────────────── │   Drawee Bank   │
│   Bank (Settled)│     Verification & Result  │  (Paying Bank)  │
└─────────────────┘                            └─────────────────┘
```
#### Technical Process
1. **Image & Data Capture:** The presenting bank scans the physical cheque to extract MICR code (Cheque Number, Routing Number, Account Number) and captures digital images (Front, Back, UV light image).
2. **Digital Signing & Encryption:** Encrypted image files are uploaded to the NCHL-ECC central server.
3. **Outward Clearing:** The central server routes cheque data to the paying (drawee) bank.
4. **Inward Verification:** The drawee bank verifies signature, balance, date, and MICR line against its Core Banking System (CBS).
5. **Net Settlement:** Approved transactions undergo Deferred Net Settlement (DNS) on the drawee/presenting bank accounts maintained at Nepal Rastra Bank.
#### Sessions and Types
* **Regular Clearing:** Standard daily clearing cycle for regular values.
* **Express / High-Value Clearing:** Fast-track sessions for high-value cheques cleared on the same business day.
* **Late / Special Clearing:** Specialized sessions tailored for urgent evening clearances.
---
### 6.2 InterBank Payment Infrastructure (IPS, connectIPS, RTGS, SWIFT)
#### 1. NCHL-IPS (Interbank Payment System)
* **Mechanism:** Batch-based **Deferred Net Settlement (DNS)** system.
* **Use Cases:** Bulk transactions such as government transfers, corporate payroll/salary, dividend payouts, social security disbursements, and loan EMIs.
* **Transaction Speed:** Non-real-time; settled in periodic clearing cycles throughout the business day.
#### 2. connectIPS
* **Mechanism:** Real-time, 24/7 account-to-account retail payment system built by NCHL.
* **Use Cases:** Citizen-to-Government (C2G) tax payments, Utility bill payments, E-commerce checkouts, and P2P direct fund transfers.
* **Features:** Links multiple BFI accounts to a single primary user profile; relies on dual-factor authentication (2FA) and device binding.
#### 3. Real Time Gross Settlement (RTGS)
* **Mechanism:** Continuous, **Real-Time** processing on a **Gross** (item-by-item) basis without netting. Owned and regulated directly by Nepal Rastra Bank.
* **Threshold:** Designed for high-value (typically NPR 2 Lakhs and above) and time-critical interbank settlements.
* **Currencies Supported:** Supports NPR as well as major foreign currencies (USD, EUR, GBP, JPY).
* **Systemic Risk Mitigation:** Eliminates credit and settlement lag risks because transactions are final and irrevocable once processed by NRB.
#### 4. SWIFT (Society for Worldwide Interbank Financial Telecommunication)
* **Mechanism:** A secure global financial messaging network; **it does not transfer actual funds** but sends standardized financial messaging instructions (using ISO 15022 / ISO 20022 formats) between institutions worldwide.
* **Key Message Types:**
* `MT103`: Single Customer Direct Wire Transfer.
* `MT202`: General Financial Institution Transfer.
* `MT700/701`: Issue of Letter of Credit (LC).
* **Security:** Uses BIC (Bank Identifier Codes / SWIFT Codes), end-to-end hardware encryption, and strict PKI access controls.

### 6.4 Digital Payment Cards
#### Structure and Card Types
1. **Debit Cards:** Directly linked to a deposit account; funds are deducted instantly upon transaction approval.
2. **Credit Cards:** Pre-approved revolving credit limit issued to customers, allowing deferred payments subject to interest-free grace periods.
3. **Prepaid Cards:** Pre-loaded card balance unconnected to a main bank account (e.g., USD Travel Cards capped under NRB exchange limits).
#### Infrastructure Components
* **Magnetic Stripe vs. EMV Chip:** Transitioned from magnet-stripe technology to **EMV (Europay, Mastercard, Visa) Chip & PIN** standards to protect against card cloning and skimming attacks.
* **Card Switches:** Infrastructure routing authorization requests between Point of Sale (POS) / ATM terminals, acquiring banks, card networks (Visa, Mastercard, UnionPay, NEPALPAY), and issuing banks.

### 6.5 Payment through Wallets
#### Operational Framework
* **PSP & PSO Licensing:** Regulates entities operating under NRB Payment and Settlement Bylaws. **Payment Service Providers (PSPs)** manage digital wallets (e.g., eSewa, Khalti), while **Payment Service Operators (PSOs)** manage payment switches and networks (e.g., NCHL, SmartChoice Technologies).
* **Stored-Value Model:** Wallets store digital value backed by equivalent cash balances held in pooled escrow accounts across licensed commercial banks.
#### Key Regulatory Controls
* **KYC (Know Your Customer):** Tiered wallet transaction limits based on verified user identity details (Unverified vs. Verified Centralized KYC).

### 6.6 Central Bank Digital Currency (CBDC)
#### Definition and Fundamental Concept
A **Central Bank Digital Currency (CBDC)** is a digital form of a country's sovereign fiat currency issued and backed directly by the central bank (Nepal Rastra Bank). Unlike commercial bank deposits or digital wallets, a CBDC represents a direct legal liability of the central bank.
#### CBDC vs. Cryptocurrencies vs. Commercial Money
| Feature | CBDC | Cryptocurrency (e.g., Bitcoin) | Commercial Bank Digital Money |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Issuer** | Central Bank (NRB) | Decentralized Network | Commercial Banks (RBB, etc.)

#### Types of CBDC Architectures
1. **Retail CBDC:** Distributed directly to the general public for daily transactions, acting as a digital alternative to physical currency notes.
2. **Wholesale CBDC:** Restricted to financial institutions (BFIs) for interbank clearing, cross-border settlements, and securities trading.
#### NRB Context and Status
Nepal Rastra Bank established a dedicated **CBDC Division** and published concept documents analyzing two-tiered retail CBDC models. Key implementation goals focus on lowering physical cash management costs, increasing financial inclusion in remote areas, and enhancing transaction transparency.
