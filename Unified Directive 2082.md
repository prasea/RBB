# 1.Provisions Relating to Minimum Capital Fund

In exercise of the powers conferred by Section 79 of the Nepal Rastra Bank Act, 2058, the following directives have been issued regarding the minimum capital fund to be maintained by Class "A", "B", and "C" licensed institutions.

#### 1. Minimum Capital Fund to be Maintained

Licensed institutions shall maintain the minimum capital fund ratio as per the following provisions:

| Institution                                                                    | Provision to Maintain Minimum Core Capital and Capital Fund             |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Class "A" institutions and Class "B" licensed national-level Development Banks | As per the **Capital Adequacy Framework, 2015**                         |
| Class "B"** and Class "C" institutions                                         | As per the **Capital Adequacy Framework, 2007 (Updated July 2008)**<br> |

* Commercial banks shall maintain a Countercyclical Buffer as provided in the Capital Adequacy Framework 2015 starting from F.Y. 2080/81. For F.Y. 2082/83, this buffer shall remain zero percent.

 Excluding national-level development banks.

#### 2. Capital Fund

"Capital Fund" refers to the sum of Core Capital (Tier 1) and Supplementary Capital (Tier 2).

Class "A" licensed institutions and Class "B" licensed national-level development banks shall calculate their capital fund as per the **Capital Adequacy Framework, 2015** set forth in attached Annex 1.1. Licensed Class "B" institutions (other than national-level) and Class "C" licensed institutions shall calculate their capital fund according to the **Capital Adequacy Framework, 2007 (Updated July 2008)** set forth in attached Annex 1.2.

#### 3. Returns/Details to be Submitted Regarding Capital Fund

(a) Licensed banks and financial institutions (BFIs) shall maintain the prescribed minimum capital fund ratio at all times.

(b) Licensed BFIs shall prepare capital fund statements based on the end-of-month financial statements in the prescribed format, get them certified by an internal auditor, and submit them through this Bank’s reporting portal within 7 days from the end of each month.

#### 4. Regarding ICAAP Guidelines

Licensed BFIs shall comply with the **Internal Capital Adequacy Assessment Process (ICAAP) Guidelines** issued by this Bank.

#### 5. Provision for Assessment/Due Diligence Audit

If this Bank deems it necessary to assess the operational status of BFIs with insufficient capital funds, it may order a **Due Diligence Audit**, the expenses of which shall be borne by the respective institution.

#### 6. Actions in Case of Non-Compliance with Capital Fund Directives

(a) If a licensed institution fails to comply with directives regarding capital funds, action shall be taken in accordance with the **Prompt Corrective Action Regulations, 2074**.

(b) Even if a licensed institution fails to maintain the prescribed minimum capital fund ratio at any point during a financial year, it cannot declare or distribute cash dividends or bonus shares based on maintaining the prescribed minimum capital fund ratio at the end of that financial year.

#### 7. Repeal and Savings

(1) The following directives previously issued by this Bank are hereby repealed:

* The provisions on minimum capital fund under Directive No. 1/081 of Unified Directives 2081, and all circulars related to this subject issued up to Magh 2, 2082.



(2) Actions taken or decisions made under the directives and circulars repealed pursuant to sub-clause (1) shall be deemed to have been taken under these directives.

# 2. Provisions Relating to Loan Disbursement, Classification, and Loan Loss Provisioning

In exercise of the powers conferred by Section 79 of the Nepal Rastra Bank Act, 2058, the following directives are issued regarding the classification of loans/advances extended by Class "A", "B", and "C" licensed institutions and the provisions to be made for potential losses on such loans.

#### 1. Classification of Loans and Advances

Licensed institutions shall classify total loans and advances based on the overdue period of principal or interest payments as follows:

##### (a) Performing Loan

Performing loans shall include the following Pass and Watchlist loans:

**(1) Pass:**

* Loans and advances that are not overdue or are overdue up to 1 month.


* Loans and advances granted against the security of Fixed Deposit Receipts (FDR).


* Loans and advances granted against the security of Government Securities and Nepal Rastra Bank Bonds.


* Gold and silver loans disbursed up to a maximum of NPR 1.0 million per customer against adequate collateral.


However, if loans are disbursed against FDRs, Government Securities, or NRB Bonds as additional collateral, or gold and silver loans exceeding NPR 1.0 million are granted, such loans shall be classified based on the overdue period.



**(2) WatchList:**

* Loans/advances overdue from 1 month up to 3 months.


* Short-term or working capital loans that are not renewed within 1 month or whose repayment period has been temporarily extended up to a maximum of 90 days.


* Loans extended to a borrower who has been classified as a Non-Performing borrower in any other Bank or Financial Institution.


* Loans extended to firms, companies, or institutions that have a negative net worth or have suffered net losses for three consecutive years, even if principal and interest payments are regular.


However, for projects under construction, this provision shall apply only after 3 full financial years of financial statements following the commercial production date.


* Multi-banking loans of NPR 2 billion or more that have not been converted into Consortium Financing.


However, this provision shall not apply to non-renewable short-term working capital loans under Pari Passu agreements.


* Loans instructed by this Bank during inspection to be placed under Watchlist based on weak cash flows and project operation status.


* Loans disbursed where the **Debt-to-Equity Ratio exceeds 80:20**. For this purpose, upon asset revaluation, any firm, company, or institution may only include assets created in full compliance with relevant Accounting Standards for Asset Revaluation as equity.


* Loans that fail to maintain the **Debt Service to Gross Income (DTI) ratio** as prescribed by this Bank.


* Loans disbursed to industries/projects/businesses that are not operational due to circumstantial reasons, provided principal and interest are being regularly paid as per prior terms.



##### (b) Non-Performing Loan (NPL)

Non-Performing Loans shall be classified into the following categories:

1. **Sub-standard:** Loans/advances overdue from 3 months up to 6 months.


2. **Doubtful:** Loans/advances overdue from 6 months up to 1 year.


3. **Loss:** Loans/advances overdue for more than 1 year.


4. Loans **rescheduled or restructured** as per Clause 8.



---

#### 2. Additional Provisions Regarding Pass Loans

Licensed institutions shall disburse or renew loans only after analyzing the minimum parameters of loan disbursement, such as the borrower's debt repayment capacity, future cash flow, and source of income, to ensure the proper utilization of the loan.

---

#### 3. Additional Provisions Regarding Loss Loans

Any loan/advance, whether overdue or not, shall be classified as a **Loss Loan** under any of the following circumstances, and BFIs must develop appropriate systems and infrastructure for this purpose:

* (a) If the borrower becomes bankrupt or is declared insolvent.


* (b) If the borrower absconds or cannot be contacted for 90 days.

* (c) If the loan is misused/diverted.

* (d) If the project/business is not in a position to operate or is non-operational, except under conditions specified in sub-clause (a)(2)(i) of Clause 1.
* (e) If Letters of Credit, Guarantees, or other contingent liabilities are converted into fund-based **Force Loans** and remain unrecovered for 90 days from the conversion date.


However, for Forced Loans arising from contractor guarantee claims that were unrecovered for 90 days in F.Y. 2080/81 but not classified as Loss, they shall be classified in F.Y. 2081/82 based on the overdue period and provisioned accordingly.

* (f) If the loan cannot be recovered and auction procedures have commenced, or legal proceedings are ongoing in court under recovery processes.

However, if the loan becomes regular after auction proceedings start, it is not mandatory to continue classifying it as Loss under this provision.
* (g) If new or additional loans are extended to a borrower listed on the Credit Information Bureau (CIB) blacklist.
* (h) If the market value of collateral is insufficient to cover the loan. (For this purpose, collateral includes not only immovable assets but also the project itself and movable project assets.)
* (i) If purchased or discounted bills are not recovered within 90 days of the due date.
* (j) If a loan in the name of one individual/firm/company is utilized by another individual/firm/company. (However, this shall not prevent group limits for firms/companies belonging to the same group.)
* (k) If a new loan is approved and disbursed for paying Trust Receipt (TR) Loans that was not disclosed when opening the Letter of Credit.
* (l) If Credit Card loans are not written off within 90 days of becoming overdue.
* (m) If the borrower submits different financial statements for the same date or period.
* (n) If a borrower takes a loan from a BFI and extends loans/advances to related individuals or institutions.



> **Clarification:** For the purpose of Clause (c) of this Directive, "misuse/diversion" shall mean not using the loan for the designated purpose, non-operation of the project, using revenues earned from the project/business for other purposes instead of paying off the debt, or where misuse is confirmed by supervisors during inspection/supervision or by auditors during an audit.

#### 4. Additional Provisions Regarding Term Loans

1. For term loans disbursed on an installment basis, if an installment becomes overdue, the total outstanding loan balance shall be classified based on the overdue period of that installment.


2. For loans extended to hydropower projects, electricity transmission lines, cable car construction projects, and renewable energy projects, if any installment is overdue by more than 90 days, the principal amount of that installment shall be classified as Loss, and a 100% loan loss provision shall be maintained for that installment.



> **Clarification:** For this purpose, "Term Loan" means loans and advances disbursed with a repayment maturity period exceeding one year.
> 
> 

---

#### 5. Additional Provisions Regarding Gold and Silver Collateral Loans

Loans against gold and silver collateral may be disbursed subject to the following provisions and conditions:

* (a) The BFI's Credit Policy/Bylaws must explicitly state provisions for granting loans against gold and silver collateral.


* (b) Prior to engaging in gold and silver loan transactions, the institution must arrange proper security measures, collateral valuation arrangements, vault insurance, and assaying/testing mechanisms.


* (c) When extending a gold and silver loan to any individual, the loan shall be disbursed only after inspecting the pledged gold or silver and ensuring loan safety. All pledged gold or silver for a borrower must be kept in a single pouch. If a loan is extended against multiple pouches, or a single pouch containing multiple sub-pouches, or without testing/assaying the metals, a **100% loan loss provision** must be created for such loans. The Single Borrower Limit (SBL) for this type of gold and silver loan is set at **NPR 5.0 million**. For previously disbursed loans exceeding this limit, the SBL must be complied with by the end of Ashadh 2083.



---

#### 6. Prohibition on Recovering Principal and Interest by Overdrawing Customer Accounts

Principal and interest on loans shall not be recovered by overdrawing a customer's current account beyond the approved Overdraft limit.

If a customer's account is debited to recover principal and interest, resulting in an overdrawn account that remains unsettled, the unrecovered principal and interest—including the overdrawn amount—shall be included in the outstanding loan balance, and such loans shall be classified **one level lower** than their current classification. However, this provision shall not restrict recovering principal and interest using existing positive balances in the customer's account.

---

#### 7. Provisions Regarding Grace Period

* (a) Generally, term loans with a grace period of more than one year shall not be granted. However, if the nature of the business or project requires a longer grace period, the approving authority may decide based on the borrower's Project Report and justification, explicitly stating the grounds and reasons. Such approval must be ratified by an authority one level higher than the original approving authority, with the Board of Directors being the highest level.


* (b) If an initially fixed grace period is extended, the loan shall be treated as **restructured/rescheduled**, and loan loss provisioning shall be maintained accordingly. However, extending the grace period due to revised Required Commercial Operation Dates (RCOD) for power projects, or because a completed hydroelectric plant cannot operate at full capacity due to incomplete transmission lines, shall **not** be considered restructuring/rescheduling.



> **Clarification:** "Grace Period" refers to the duration between the loan disbursement date and the date installment payments commence.
> 
> 

---

#### 8. Provisions Regarding Rescheduling and Restructuring of Loans

1. **"Rescheduling"** refers to the process of extending the maturity period/time for repayment of a loan facility.


2. **"Restructuring"** refers to the process of altering the nature or terms of a loan facility, or adding/modifying covenants.


3. If a borrower exhibits a decline in debt service capacity, licensed institutions may reschedule and/or restructure the loan if convinced by the written action plan submitted by the borrower based on the following:


* (a) Evidence of adequate loan documentation and security/collateral.


* (b) Grounds convincing the institution of loan recovery prospects and assurance of business continuity through rescheduling/restructuring.


* (c) Submission of a written action plan along with the recovery of at least **25% of the total accrued outstanding interest** up to the date of rescheduling/restructuring.




4. A borrower’s debt service capacity shall be considered diminished under any of the following conditions:


1. Installments or interest are not paid on due dates due to temporary revenue drops caused by Cost Overruns, Time Overruns, or Cash Flow Mismatches.


2. Future installments or interest cannot be paid on schedule due to adverse external conditions or other factors, despite current regular payments.


3. The borrowing company is delisted or in the process of being delisted from Nepal Stock Exchange Ltd.


4. Actual or projected financial conditions are insufficient for installment or interest payments.





(Sub-clauses 5 through 15 detail specific sector-wise temporary restructuring/rescheduling schemes including real estate, Gen-Z movement disruptions, natural disasters/landslides, earthquakes, customs blockades, MSME/agricultural loans, and construction contractors, subject to specific deadline dates, minimum interest recovery requirements, and non-write-back rules for provisions).

16. Clear justifications for loan rescheduling or restructuring must be attached to each credit file.


17. Accrued outstanding interest shall **not** be capitalized during loan rescheduling or restructuring.


18. Separate records/statements of loans classified under Sub-clause (3) shall be maintained.

#### 9. Provisions for Loan Loss Reserve / Provisioning

1. Based on loan classification and outstanding principal balance, minimum Loan Loss Provisions shall be maintained as follows:



| Loan Classification  | Minimum Loan Loss Provision |
| -------------------- | --------------------------- |
| **(a) Pass**         | 1%                          |
| **(b) Watchlist**    | 5%                          |
| **(c) Sub-standard** | 25%                         |
| **(d) Doubtful**     | 50%                         |
| **(e) Loss**         | 100%                        |


2. For loans classified under Non-Performing categories (excluding restructured/rescheduled loans), upon full payment of all overdue principal and interest, the loan shall remain in its respective NPL category (Sub-standard, Doubtful, or Loss) for **3 consecutive months of regular payments** before it can be upgraded, after which the corresponding lower provision rate shall apply.
3. If a corporate borrower holds multiple credit facilities and any single facility is classified as NPL, all other performing facilities of that borrower must be classified as **at least Watchlist**.
4. If an individual borrower holds multiple credit facilities and any single facility is classified as NPL, all other performing facilities of that individual must be classified as **at least Watchlist**.
5. If a loan taken by one borrower within an interconnected group is classified as NPL, other group members directly impacted by business interdependencies shall also be classified in the **same NPL category**.



(Sub-clauses 6–14 provide specific rules for grace-period provisioning in infrastructure/agriculture, third-party collateral penalties [+20% additional provision], personal guarantees, bank guarantees, margin loans, and credit card provisions).

---

#### 10. Conditions for Adjustment/Reversal of Loan Loss Provision

Loan Loss Provisions shall not be adjusted or written back except under the following conditions:

* (a) When the loan is written off.
* (b) When partial or installment payments are received, to the extent of the provision maintained for the paid amount.
* (c) When principal and interest payments for restructured or rescheduled loans remain regular for two consecutive years.
* (d) When the loan is fully settled/repaid.
* (e) When issues identified during supervisory inspections are rectified to the satisfaction of the concerned Supervision Department.



---

#### 11. Non-Banking Assets (NBA), Provisioning, and Auction Sales

1. If a loan remains unrecovered, the BFI may sell the mortgaged collateral to recover outstanding principal and interest. The BFI shall assume ownership (Non-Banking Asset - NBA) only if the collateral fails to sell after completing auction processes at least **3 times**. Upon assuming NBA, it must be valued at the **prevailing market value or the total outstanding dues up to the preceding day, whichever is lower**. If the market value is lower than total dues, the shortfall must be charged as an expense to the Profit & Loss account in the same financial year.


2. Interest accrued shall not be booked as income until full settlement of the NBA; such interest must be moved to the NBA Loss Reserve.

3. When acquiring assets as NBA or auctioning acquired NBAs:
* (a) For borrowers with outstanding dues exceeding **NPR 1.0 million**, the BFI must mandatorily list the party on the CIB blacklist upon acquiring their asset as NBA. For dues up to NPR 1.0 million, blacklisting is at the discretion of the BFI.
* (b) Independent realistic valuations must be conducted prior to auctioning NBAs. The same valuator cannot evaluate the same asset more than once sequentially.
* (c) NBAs must be liquidated as quickly as possible. If required for the BFI's own operational use, Board approval and notification to NRB are required.





---

#### 12. Guidelines for Framing Loan Write-Off Bylaws

1. BFIs shall formulate Write-Off Bylaws through their Board of Directors outlining criteria for unrecoverable loans, subject to approval from NRB.


2. Loans may be written off under the following conditions:
* (a) The loan must be classified as Loss with a **100% provision** maintained.
* (b) Auction procedures for mortgaged collateral must have been completed as per prevailing laws.
* (c) The collateral must have been assumed as NBA if auction sales failed.
* (d) The borrower and related parties must be listed on the CIB blacklist. (Exemption applies to write-offs under NPR 200,000).


3. A separate **Credit Recovery Unit** must be established to pursue ongoing recovery efforts for written-off loans.

---

#### 13. Implementation of Supreme Court Verdicts

BFIs must strictly observe directives issued by the Supreme Court regarding:

* Establishing clear, explicit liability limits for personal guarantors.
* Legible credit and guarantee contract terms.
* Exhausting recovery from primary borrowers before enforcing guarantor collateral.
* Objective, realistic collateral valuations avoiding arbitrary undervaluation.
* Empathetic, respectful recovery processes that preserve human dignity and family reputation.
* Avoiding frequent, arbitrary interest rate hikes contrary to initial agreements.



---

#### 14. Credit Sale / Purchase / Re-purchase and Takeover (CS, CP, RP & TO)

* BFIs without adequate capital funds as prescribed by NRB cannot engage in CS, CP, RP, or Takeover transactions without prior approval.


* BFIs with adequate capital funds may engage in CS, CP, RP, and Takeovers provided their credit policies permit it, excluding the final month of the financial year.


* Credit purchases require explicit customer consent and complete transfer of recovery rights.



---

#### 17. Margin Lending against Shares Listed on Security Exchanges

* Loans against shares of listed companies may be extended up to **70% of the lower of**:
* The 180-day average closing price published by NEPSE, OR
* The prevailing market price.




* No additional credit limit may be granted by revaluing pledged shares post-disbursement.


* If market value falls, BFIs must issue **Margin Calls**.


* Total Margin Lending exposure cannot exceed **40% of the BFI's Core Capital**. Pledged shares of delisted, loss-making (3 consecutive years), negative net worth, or distressed companies cannot be accepted as collateral.



---

#### 26. Personal Overdrafts and Unspecified Personal Loans

* Unspecified personal overdrafts, mortgage loans, property loans, personal term loans, and margin loans are capped at a maximum of **NPR 10.0 million per individual across the entire banking system**.


* For new, renewed, or extended personal loans secured against real estate, the maximum **Loan-to-Value (LTV) ratio shall not exceed 50%** based on Fair Market Value.



---

#### 27. Requirement of Permanent Account Number (PAN)

BFIs must mandate a **Permanent Account Number (PAN)** for any individual borrower taking single or aggregate credit facilities of **NPR 2.5 million or more**. If a borrower belongs to an interconnected group holding NPR 2.5 million or more in total loans, PAN is mandatory even if the individual's loan is below NPR 2.5 million.

---

#### 49. Debt Service to Gross Income (DTI) Ratio

BFIs shall enforce maximum Debt Service to Gross Income (DTI) limits for personal loans:

* **Non-business personal loans / Overdrafts / Auto loans:** Maximum DTI limit of **50%**.


* **Residential Home Loans (Purchase/Construction):** Maximum DTI limit of **70%**.


* Loans exceeding these DTI limits must be classified as **Watchlist**.

# 3. Provisions Relating to Single Obligor Limits and Sectoral Credit Exposure

In exercise of the powers conferred by Section 79 of the Nepal Rastra Bank Act, 2058, the following directives are issued regarding exposure limits to a single customer, firm, company, or interconnected group, as well as sectoral limits.

#### 1. Single Obligor Limit (SOL)

The maximum limit of credit facilities that a licensed institution may grant to a single customer, firm, company, or group of interconnected borrowers is termed the **Single Obligor Limit (SOL)**.

#### 2. Determination of Credit Limits

* BFIs may extend fund-based and non-fund-based facilities up to a maximum of **25% of Core Capital** to a single customer or group.


* For priority sectors—such as export industries, SMEs, transport infrastructure, pharmaceutical manufacturing, agriculture, tourism, cement, iron, and manufacturing—the SOL cap is raised up to **30% of Core Capital**.


* Core Capital figures shall be based on the un-audited/audited balance sheet of the preceding quarter certified by the internal auditor.



#### 3. Hydropower, Transmission Lines, Renewable Energy, and Cable Car Projects

* BFIs may extend credit facilities (fund-based and non-fund-based combined) up to **50% of Core Capital** for pure hydroelectricity, transmission line, renewable energy, and cable car projects.


* A valid **Power Purchase Agreement (PPA)** with relevant authorities is mandatory if exposure exceeds 25% of Core Capital.


* If a borrower in this sector also seeks financing for non-energy/non-cable-car projects, total combined exposure must not exceed 50%, with non-energy exposure capped at a maximum of 25%.



#### 4. Exemptions from Single Obligor Limits

SOL provisions shall **not apply** to:

* Loans secured against Fixed Deposit Receipts, Government Securities, or NRB Bonds.


* Unconditional guarantees from international multilateral financial institutions (World Bank, ADB, IFC) or rated international banks (Top 1000 World Banks listed in *The Banker* magazine).


* Credit facilities extended to **Nepal Oil Corporation, Food Management and Trading Company, Agricultural Inputs Company Ltd., and Salt Trading Corporation** for importing essential commodities (petroleum, food grains, chemical fertilizers).



#### 6. Interconnected Borrowers / Group Definition

For SOL evaluation, borrowers are treated as a **Single Group** if:

* One company holds **25% or more shares** in another company.


* Directors, major shareholders, partners, or proprietors are shared, or share undivided family/household relationships.



# 4. Arrangements Relating to Accounting Policies and Formats of Financial Statements

In exercise of the powers conferred by Section 79 of the Nepal Rastra Bank Act, 2058 (2002), the following directives are hereby issued regarding the accounting policies and formats of financial statements to be observed by Class "A", "B", and "C" licensed institutions.

#### 1. Arrangements Relating to Financial Statements

Licensed institutions shall prepare financial statements in accordance with the following provisions:

##### A. Financial Year

"Financial Year" shall mean the period from the 1st of Shrawan of each year to the end of Ashad (Ashad Masanta) of the following year. However, for the year of establishment, the period from the date of establishment to the end of Ashad shall be considered the financial year.

##### B. Format Prescribed by NRB to be Treated as Statutory Form

Financial statements shall be prepared strictly according to the format and method prescribed by this Bank.

##### C. Compliance with Applicable Accounting Standards

Licensed institutions shall prepare their financial statements in accordance with Nepal Financial Reporting Standards (NFRS). In areas not covered by NFRS, International Financial Reporting Standards (IFRS) shall be used as the basis.

##### D. Publication of Annual Financial Statements

After completion of the audit within the legally stipulated timeframe, the financial statements shall be published in the annual report booklet, and the booklet must be updated on the institution’s website. Furthermore, for public information, the Statement of Financial Position, Statement of Profit or Loss, Statement of Comprehensive Income, Statement of Cash Flows, Statement of Changes in Equity, the Capital Fund Schedule as per Directive No. 1, and Key Financial Indicators shall be mandatorily published in a national-level daily newspaper within the timeframe specified in the Banks and Financial Institutions Act, 2073 (2017).

##### E. Publication of Interim Financial Reports

1. Banks and financial institutions shall mandatorily publish an interim financial report (condensed report)—including consolidated financial reports and Major Financial Indicators—in the format prescribed for this purpose in a national-level daily newspaper within 15 days from the end of each quarter. A copy thereof must be submitted to both the Bank and Financial Institution Regulation Department and the concerned Supervision Department. Additionally, detailed interim financial reports in the prescribed format must be hosted on their official websites.
The fact that detailed interim financial reports are available on the website must be explicitly stated in the newspaper publication.
*However, the 4th quarter interim financial report (condensed report) shall be published within 30 days from the end of the financial year.*
2. Interim financial reports must be published only after mandatory certification by the internal auditor.
3. At the end of each financial year, any discrepancy observed between the unadjusted interim financial report and the audited financial statements of the institution must be disclosed with explanations in a tabular format on a separate page of the institution's annual report, using the format prescribed by this Bank.
4. When publishing interim financial reports, all expenses—including staff bonus, employee-related expenses, income tax, and regulatory adjustments—for that period must be fully incorporated. Furthermore, Earnings Per Share (EPS) must be published as Annualized EPS.
5. Unadjusted interim financial reports published for public information must carry a mandatory footnote stating: *"The adjusted financial statements may differ if the supervisory authority and external auditors direct additions/modifications."*
6. If differences arise in financial reporting due to the management's failure to make timely adjustments on matters explicitly clarified in Acts, policies, rules, and directives, necessary action shall be initiated against the concerned institution.

##### F. Regarding the Purpose of this Directive

Loans, deposits, and other assets and liabilities recognized, evaluated, and presented in accordance with the bank's accounting policy pursuant to this directive shall be used solely for financial statement presentation. Statistics required to be submitted to or published by various departments of this Bank under different directives shall continue to be governed by those respective directives. Nothing in this directive shall be deemed to obstruct any other directives concerning data forms and details required to be submitted or published to various departments/offices of this Bank.

##### G. Accounting Statements Prepared for Internal Purposes

Nothing in this directive shall be deemed to prevent licensed institutions from preparing other accounting statements for internal use.

##### H. Prohibition of Changes to Prescribed Formats and Headings

Even if there is zero balance under any heading, the heading must be retained and no changes to the prescribed format shall be made. However, additional line items may be added as necessary under the "Others" schedule.

##### I. Submission of Annual Financial Statements

Based on annual accounts, after completion of preliminary audit work by the external auditor, the preliminary report, management's response thereto, and financial statements prepared in the NRB-prescribed format shall be submitted to the concerned Supervision Department for approval within 3 months from the end of the financial year. If off-site supervision directs any amendments, adjustment must be made and the audit completed within 4 months from the end of the financial year (or within a maximum extended period of up to 2 additional months, if extended). The adjusted financial statements, final auditor's report, and Long Form Audit Report (LFAR) must be submitted to the concerned Supervision Department within 15 days of audit completion.

Annual Accounts prepared for presentation at the Annual General Meeting (AGM) shall be made public only after obtaining concurrence from the concerned Supervision Department of this Bank.

---

#### 2. Basis of Preparation and Significant Accounting Policies

(a) Financial statements shall be prepared in compliance with Nepal Financial Reporting Standards (NFRS). In areas not covered by NFRS, International Financial Reporting Standards (IFRS) shall serve as the basis.

(b) Accounting policies adopted during preparation shall be approved by the Board of Directors of the licensed institution subject to NFRS provisions. Explanatory details of accounting policies adopted shall be disclosed under the "Significant Accounting Policies" section in the Notes to Accounts. Any change in accounting policies must adhere to NFRS and be disclosed under "Changes in Accounting Policies" under the "Basis of Preparation" section in the Notes to Accounts.

---

#### 3. Actuarial Valuation of Employee Obligations

When conducting Actuarial Valuations of employee obligations under NFRS provisions, institutions shall base estimations on realistic assumptions supported by historical data. Furthermore, actuarial valuation shall be arranged only after such data and assumptions are verified by internal audit.

---

#### 4. Regulatory Adjustments

From net profits derived under adopted accounting policies, licensed institutions shall allocate funds to statutory and other reserves as mandated by the Banks and Financial Institutions Act, 2073 and NRB directives. Regulatory adjustments shall be made from the remaining profit as detailed below, and dividends may only be distributed from the residual amount:

(a) When preparing the first NFRS-compliant financial statements, if amounts were credited to retained earnings by making adjustments across various line items in the Opening Statement of Financial Position and previous year's financial statements under NFRS, an equivalent amount must be debited from Retained Earnings via the Statement of Changes in Equity and credited to the Regulatory Reserve.

(b) For the first NFRS-compliant financial statement and subsequent years, regulatory adjustments shall be debited/credited to the Regulatory Reserve as follows:

**(A) (1)** Out of loan interest income recognized as per the institution's accounting policy, for interest recognized on an accrual basis as per NRB directives that remains uncollected, the net amount after deducting applicable income tax, staff bonus, and statutory funds (General Reserve and CSR Fund) shall be debited from Retained Earnings and credited to the Regulatory Reserve. Upon actual recovery of such interest, the amount may be transferred back from the Regulatory Reserve to Retained Earnings.

**(2)** Regarding interest capitalization on loans sanctioned and achieving Financial Closure after the issuance date of this directive (2079/10/26 B.S.):

#### 5. Accounting for Mergers and Acquisitions (M&A)

When two or more institutions merge or one institution acquires another, the acquiring/merged entity shall account for assets, liabilities, and equity of the target entity as follows:

# 5. Arrangements Relating to Risk Management

In exercise of the powers conferred by Section 79 of the Nepal Rastra Bank Act, 2058, this directive is issued regarding the management of risks—including credit, operational, liquidity, market, interest rate, and foreign exchange risks—inherent in the operations of Class "A", "B", and "C" licensed institutions.

#### 1. Risk Management

Institutions must establish and execute a robust risk management framework to identify, measure, monitor, manage, control, and report all risks faced. An enterprise-wide integrated approach must be adopted for comprehensive risk management. Institutions must demonstrate full commitment to risk management by establishing an effective alignment between corporate governance and robust risk governance.

#### 2. Risk Management Program

1. The risk management framework maintained by an institution shall minimally encompass:
* (a) Active oversight by the Board of Directors (BOD) and Senior Management;
* (b) Adequacy of policies and procedural systems;
* (c) An effective, independent Risk Management Function;
* (d) Appropriate Management Information Systems (MIS); and
* (e) Effective internal control systems and limit structures.


2. The Board of Directors shall formulate risk strategies by defining the institution's Risk Appetite and Tolerance Limits. The Board remains ultimate responsible for enterprise risk management.
3. Policies and procedures to address Material Risks must be approved by the BOD and reviewed at least annually (and updated as needed). Senior Management is responsible for implementing these risk policies and procedures. Both the BOD and Senior Management shall review and discuss risk reports at least quarterly and whenever deemed necessary.
4. Institutions shall establish an independent Risk Management Function under a Chief Risk Officer (CRO) or equivalent position. Depending on institutional size and complexity, separate or combined risk divisions/units may be set up. The Risk Management Function must minimally cover:
* (a) Managing the formulation and periodic updating of risk policies/procedures;
* (b) Regularly monitoring material risks across all operational units and alerting decision-makers on unanticipated risks;
* (c) Preparing risk reports and presenting them to the Risk Management Committee and BOD; and
* (d) Facilitating effective execution across all aspects of risk management.


5. Roles, duties, and powers of the CRO shall be explicitly defined to match organizational scale and complexity. The CRO shall report directly and periodically to the Board-level Risk Management Committee.
6. Business functions and risk management functions must be separated to prevent conflicts of interest. However, risk management must not be viewed solely as the responsibility of the Risk Management Department; the primary business function serves as the **First Line of Defence**. A strong **Risk Culture** must be cultivated where every employee is conscious of their risk management responsibilities.
7. Strong Internal Control Systems must be maintained to keep operational risks within approved Risk Appetite and Tolerance Limits. The Board/Risk Management Committee shall commit to conducting effective audits to ensure internal control robustness.
8. Institutions shall prepare an annual report covering all implemented risk policies, procedures, the CRO mandate, and the Risk Governance Structure, submitting it to the concerned Supervision Department within 1 month from the end of the financial year.

#### 3. Classification of Risks

For effective risk management, institutions shall categorize risks into:

* (a) Credit Risk
* (b) Operational Risk
* (c) Liquidity Risk
* (d) Market Risk
* (e) Interest Rate Risk
* (f) Foreign Exchange Risk
* (g) Other Risks

#### 4. Provisions Relating to Credit Risk Management

1. Directive No. 2 (*Credit Classification and Loan Loss Provisioning*), Directive No. 3 (*Single Obligor & Sectoral Credit Limits*), and Directive No. 8 (*Investment Management*) shall serve as fundamental guidelines for mitigating credit and investment risks.
2. Appropriate policies and procedures capable of identifying, measuring, monitoring, and controlling credit risk must be adopted.
3. Overall credit authority structures must be approved by the BOD. Delegated credit authorities to Senior Management must be clear and aligned with scale/complexity.
4. Functions such as Business Solicitation, Credit Assessment, Credit Approval, Disbursement, and Relationship Management must be segregated.
5. Thorough credit evaluation standards must be established before issuing new loans, renewing facilities, or enhancing limits. Institutions must verify and ensure that credit utilization strictly aligns with stated borrowing purposes.
6. Class "A" Commercial Banks and National-Level Class "B" Development Banks must internally develop an independent **Credit Risk Rating System**. This system must assess borrower/business creditworthiness and serve as a core component of Borrower Due Diligence, drawing upon international best practices.
7. Appropriate **Credit Administration Functions** must be established for maintaining credit documentation, repayment monitoring, loan file management, collateral records, and borrower profile updates.
8. Periodic portfolio reviews (both single-borrower and sectoral) must be conducted to inform customer relationship management strategies.
9. Institutions are encouraged to set internal risk limits (e.g., sectoral, purpose-wise, NPL caps) aligned with BOD-approved Risk Appetite, Tolerance Limits, and regulatory constraints.
10. Sound policies and procedures must be implemented for the proactive management and resolution of Non-Performing Loans (NPLs).
11. In Consortium Financing, the Lead Bank carries primary responsibility for credit risk analysis; however, participating banks must conduct independent assessments.
12. Licensed institutions shall accept **only** audit reports featuring a valid Unique Document Identification Number (UDIN). Verification of details via the ICAN UDIN portal must be formally documented in loan files.

#### 5. Provisions Relating to Operational Risk Management

* (a) Policies and procedures must cover risks arising from people, processes, systems, and external events across all operating units (accounting, IT, HR, cash management, and core banking).
* (b) Effective internal controls and information systems must be developed. Internal auditors must evaluate operational risk controls in branch/department audit reports.
* (c) A structured system must track operational loss incidents (nature, severity, financial impact) alongside regular risk monitoring.
* (d) Robust **Disaster Recovery (DR)** and **Business Continuity Plans (BCP)** must be formulated and tested through mandatory annual drills.
* (e) Key-person dependencies, single-staff tenure risks, and collusion risks must be mitigated via structured rotation and transfer policies.
* (f) A **Contingency Management Framework** must be implemented to protect operational continuity against natural disasters and systemic shocks.
* (g) To enhance transactional security, institutions shall issue **only Chip-based** Debit, Credit, and Prepaid cards.

#### 6. Provisions Relating to Liquidity Risk Management

1. A clear Liquidity Risk Policy with operational procedures must be implemented based on institutional scale and complexity.
2. Assets and liabilities must be mapped into maturity buckets (Time Intervals). Liquidity Profiles (Form No. 5.1) must be submitted quarterly to the Supervision Department within 15 days of quarter-end (Ashwin, Poush, Chaitra, and Ashad).
3. Maturity time intervals shall be categorized as:
* (a) Up to 90 days
* (b) Over 90 days up to 180 days
* (c) Over 180 days up to 270 days
* (d) Over 270 days up to 1 year
* (e) Over 1 year


4. Fixed maturity assets and liabilities shall be mapped directly into corresponding time buckets.
5. Non-fixed maturity liabilities (such as current and savings accounts—CASA) representing core deposits shall be mapped into the "Over 1 year" bucket up to the minimum stable balance threshold. Institutions shall make realistic internal estimates of core current deposits.
6. The maximum ceiling for the **Credit-Deposit Ratio (CD Ratio)** is capped at **90%**.
*Explanations:*
* Capital fund components, debentures (excluding those counted in capital funds), foreign currency borrowings over 1-year tenure, and NRB refinance facilities may be counted as deposits. Debenture treatment must be declared at the time of issuance.
* For debentures issued prior to 2078/05/08 B.S., 100% of the value could be counted as deposits up to Poush-end 2080, and 50% until Ashad-end 2081.
* If the CD Ratio exceeds 90%, it must be gradually reduced to meet compliance. Daily tracking and monthly average monitoring must be established under a BOD-approved action plan submitted to NRB.
* Interbank deposits (funds lent from one licensed institution deposited into another) **shall not** be included as deposits for CD Ratio calculations.


7. **CD Ratio Monitoring & Penalties:**
* CD Ratio is monitored daily.
* If monthly average CD Ratio exceeds 90%, monetary penalties based on the prevailing Bank Rate shall be levied via the Supervision Department.
* Monthly average ratios are calculated using all business days (excluding public holidays) in a calendar month.
* Formula for monthly penalty calculation:
$$\text{Penalty} = \frac{(\text{Monthly Avg Credit Outstanding} - \text{Monthly Avg Deposit} \times 90\%) \times \text{Bank Rate}}{100 \times 12}$$


* *Exemption:* Penalties shall not apply to institutions maintaining a Net Liquid Assets to Total Deposit ratio of **22% or higher** from Poush 1, 2079 B.S.


8. An **Asset Liability Committee (ALCO)** must meet regularly to manage liquidity positions and maintain risk parameters within tolerance levels.
9. A **Contingency Funding Plan (CFP)** must be established to navigate liquidity stress scenarios.
10. ALCO minutes and liquidity risk reports must be reviewed quarterly by the Board of Directors.
11. Institutions are encouraged to establish internal liquidity buffers (CRR, SLR, CD ratio caps) suited to their operational profile.

#### 7. Provisions Relating to Market Risk Management

1. Necessary policies and procedures must address material market risks stemming from fluctuations in prices and interest rates.
2. Class "A" Commercial Banks must establish an independent **Middle Office** within Treasury Management. The Middle Office shall operate independently of Treasury front-office trading functions and report to the Risk Management Department/Unit.

#### 8. Provisions Relating to Interest Rate Risk Management

1. Institutions must establish policies to measure and mitigate interest rate volatility risks.
2. **Mitigation Measures:**
* (a) Only Interest Sensitive Assets (ISA) and Interest Sensitive Liabilities (ISL) shall be included in gap calculations.
* (b) Non-interest-bearing accounts (e.g., cash balances, zero-interest accounts) must be excluded from Maturity Mismatch Gap Analysis.
* (c) Interest Rate Risk reports (Form No. 5.2) must be submitted quarterly within 15 days of quarter-end.


3. Time buckets for interest rate risk tracking must strictly follow Form 5.2 parameters.
4. Non-fixed maturity assets/liabilities shall be mapped as follows:
* **Assets:** Floating rate loans with periodic interest rate adjustments shall be placed in the time bucket matching their repricing frequency. Loans tied to external benchmark rates (e.g., Treasury Bill yields) shall be categorized in the shortest time bucket.
* **Liabilities:** Interest-sensitive floating rate liabilities without fixed maturity shall be bucketed according to their repricing frequency.


5. **Gap Calculation Methodology:**
* $\text{Gap} = \text{Total Assets} - \text{Total Liabilities}$ (per time bucket; can be positive or negative).
* **Cumulative Gap** must be calculated across successive time buckets to evaluate total exposure.
* Potential interest rate shifts (typically evaluated using a baseline 1% rate shock) must be modeled.
* Adjusted Interest Rate Change ($IRC$) for a given time interval ($d$ days) is calculated as:
$$IRC = \frac{d}{365} \times \Delta i$$


*(Example: For a 1% rate change over a 90-day bucket, $IRC = \frac{90}{365} \times 0.01 = 0.002465$)*
* The earnings impact on Profit & Loss is computed by multiplying the Cumulative Gap by the adjusted $IRC$.


6. Before launching new products, institutions must assess interest rate sensitivity impacts on the balance sheet. Advanced pricing, repricing, and competitive analyses must be executed.

#### 9. Provisions Relating to Foreign Exchange Risk Management

1. Policies must govern potential risks to income and capital arising from foreign exchange rate fluctuations.
2. Foreign Exchange Risk Reports (Form No. 5.3) must be submitted monthly within 7 days of month-end.
3. Institutions shall maintain an **Exchange Fluctuation Reserve** as mandated by Directive No. 4.
4. Foreign currency holdings must be segregated by currency into short-term (1 month or less) and long-term positions to determine **Net Open Positions (NOP)**.
5. The daily Net Open Position in foreign currencies is capped at a maximum of **30% of Core Capital (Tier 1)** from the previous quarter. If exceeded, the institution must bring NOP within limits within 1 month; failure to do so shall invite enforcement action under Section 100 of the NRB Act, 2058. Foreign Currency Deposit Liabilities and forward/derivative positions must be adjusted in NOP calculations.
6. Institutions must maintain capabilities to evaluate, measure, and hedge both on-balance-sheet and off-balance-sheet foreign exchange risks.

#### 10. Management of Other Risks

Institutions must establish risk identification and mitigation policies for non-financial risks including **Reputation Risk, Strategic Risk, AML/CFT Risk, Legal Risk, and Environmental, Social, and Climate Change (ESRM) Risks**.

#### 11. Risk Management Guidelines

Institutions shall adopt and enforce all supplementary **Risk Management Guidelines** issued by NRB to strengthen internal controls.

#### 12. Stress Testing Guidelines

Institutions must perform mandatory, regular **Stress Testing** in accordance with NRB Stress Testing Guidelines:

* (a) Quarterly stress test results must be reviewed and deliberated by the Board and Senior Management.
* (b) The official NRB Stress Testing Spreadsheet must be obtained from the Off-Site Supervision Division.
* (c) Completed stress test spreadsheets and results must be submitted to the Off-Site Supervision Division within 30 days of quarter-end.

#### 13. Repeal and Savings

1. Directive No. 5/081 of Unified Directives 2081 and related circulars issued up to Magh 2, 2082 B.S. are hereby repealed.
2. Actions taken under repealed directives shall be deemed executed under this directive.

# 6. Arrangements Relating to Corporate Governance

In exercise of the powers conferred by Section 79 of the Nepal Rastra Bank Act, 2058, the following directives are issued regarding corporate governance standards to be maintained by Class "A", "B", and "C" licensed institutions.

#### 1. Code of Conduct for Directors

1. **Minimum Acceptable Conduct:**
* (a) Directors must sign a commitment letter pledging adherence to NRB conduct standards.
* (b) Upon appointment, directors must declare all personal financial interests involving the institution in writing to the Board before assuming duty.
* (c) Directors shall not interfere in daily operational management.
* (d) Directors acting beyond their delegated authority shall be personally liable; the institution shall not bear liability for ultra vires actions.
* (e) Directors must exhibit professional, courteous, and ethical behavior. Workplace harassment, hostility, or misconduct is strictly prohibited.


2. **Prohibition of Conflict of Interest:**
* (a) Directors shall not engage in activities directly or indirectly detrimental to institutional interests.
* (b) Directors must recuse themselves from transactions involving personal financial interest. Directors holding ownership, partnership, or guarantor status with borrowing clients are disqualified from holding directorship.
* (c) Public trading of listed shares/debentures is exempt from this restriction unless financial conflict of interest is formally established by the Board.
* (d) Undivided family members of directors holding >10% equity in securities firms (brokerages, market makers) shall not engage in securities transactions on behalf of the bank.
* (e) Institutions shall not rent real estate property from firms/companies owned by directors or shareholders holding >1% equity (or their family members).


3. **Prohibition of Dual Positions:** No director may simultaneously serve as a director, advisor, or executive in another licensed financial institution, cooperative, or insurance company.
4. **Fiduciary Restraint:** Directors shall not act as administrators or guardians in customer property/real estate transactions without explicit written approval from the Board.
5. **Prohibition of Misuse of Office:** Directors shall not misuse their official position for personal gain or accord undue priority to family members in loans, procurement, or treasury dealings (gold, FX, securities).
6. **Accuracy of Records:** Directors shall maintain complete, accurate accounting records and are strictly barred from altering official documentation.
7. **Confidentiality:** Client confidentiality must be preserved during and after the director's tenure.
8. **Impartiality:** Professional dealings must remain objective, impartial, and free from personal relationships.
9. **Notification:** The institution must submit written confirmation of compliance with code-of-conduct provisions to NRB.
10. **Reporting:** Annual compliance reports regarding directors' conduct must be submitted to NRB, detailing any violations and penalties enforced.
11. **Board Attendance:** Directors must attend Board meetings regularly (physical or virtual). Unexcused absence from more than 3 consecutive meetings results in automatic vacation of office.
12. **Cross-Holding Restrictions:** No family member, firm, or group of companies may nominate more than one director/CEO in a single institution.
13. **Public Servants:** Sitting government employees or active NRB/bank staff cannot serve as directors, except in Microfinance ("D" class) or Infrastructure Development Banks under BAFIA provisions.
14. **Blacklisting Disqualification:** Individuals associated with blacklisted firms are disqualified from directorship during the blacklisting period and for 3 years post-removal.
15. **Promoter Directorship:** Promoter shareholders can run for directorship only within the promoter category.
16. **Multiple Directorships Prohibited:** No individual or entity representative may hold multiple directorships simultaneously.
17. **Elected Representatives:** Elected officials across local, provincial, or federal governments are ineligible to serve on bank boards during their term in office.
18. **Age Limits:** Individuals aged  over 70 years cannot be appointed/reappointed as directors. No director may remain in office past **74 years of age**.
19. **Voting Restrictions at General Meetings:** Shareholders/directors shall not vote on matters where personal financial conflicts, default status, or regulatory sanctions apply.

#### 2. Duties and Responsibilities of the Board of Directors

* (a) Formulate transparent Human Resource Bylaws and recruit qualified Senior Management.
* (b) Ensure periodic internal audits. Outsourced internal audits must be assigned to certified professionals, overseen by an officer acting as Member Secretary to the Audit Committee. Internal audit reports must be submitted directly to the Audit Committee quarterly.
* (c) Maintain strict independence between Internal Auditors and Statutory Auditors—firms/partners cannot perform both roles concurrently for the same institution.
* (d) Monitor operations prudently to safeguard public deposit funds.
* (e) Oversee delegated staff activities; maintain records of disciplinary actions enforced by the Board.
* (f) Formulate comprehensive written policies covering investments, credit, ALM, budgeting, and operations.
* (g) Exercise active oversight and take prompt corrective measures when operational red flags emerge.
* (h) Ensure strict compliance with all applicable laws, NRB regulations, and circulars.
* (i) Directors acting against depositor interests or failing to produce documents requested by NRB shall pay personal fines levied under Section 100 of the NRB Act, 2058. Institutions are barred from reimbursing such fines.
* (j) Promoters, directors, or shareholders holding >0.1% equity (including family holdings) cannot serve as **Independent Directors**.
* (k) Non-executive directors shall not perform daily executive management tasks. Board letterheads/seals shall not be used for daily routine correspondence.
* (l) Non-directors invited to Board meetings shall not receive director meeting fees, with exceptions for independent domain experts providing formal advisory opinions.
* (m) Directors must take the oath of office and secrecy within **35 days** of appointment.
* (n) Executive compensation and perks must be aligned with NRB-issued executive remuneration guidelines and BAFIA Section 120 limits.
* (o) Institutional recruitment via talent hunts must follow transparent, formalized procedures.

#### 3. Director Education Program

1. Newly appointed directors must undergo an orientation program within **1 month**, covering governance, risk strategy, financial condition, regulations, and fiduciary duties.
2. Refresher orientation programs covering global best practices (transparency, compliance, conflict management) must be conducted at least **once a year** for all directors.
3. Formulated Director Education Bylaws must be submitted to NRB Regulation and Supervision Departments.

#### 4. Provisions Relating to Chief Executive Officer (CEO) Appointment

1. CEOs shall be appointed under BAFIA and internal bylaws. To maintain checks and balances, the **Board Chairman shall not serve as CEO**.
2. **Age Limits:** Individuals aged **over 65 years** cannot be appointed/reappointed as CEO. No CEO may remain in office past **69 years of age**.
3. **Qualifications:** CEOs must possess proven banking experience, management capability, clear police records (no criminal background), no history of blacklisting, and clean regulatory standing.
4. NRB must be immediately notified of any CEO appointment, resignation, dismissal, or Board reconstitution.
5. NRB must be informed in advance if the CEO travels abroad or takes leave exceeding 7 days, specifying the acting CEO designation.
6. CEO remuneration contracts must state all terms transparently, avoiding ambiguous wording.
7. Executive Directors/CEOs cannot hold dual employment in other commercial entities.
8. Shareholding caps for CEO eligibility: Individuals holding equity exceeding 1% in Class "A", 2% in Class "B", or 5% in Class "C" institutions are **ineligible** to serve as CEO or employee.
9. CEOs of regional institutions must remain regularly present at head offices.
10. CEOs are restricted to a maximum of **two consecutive terms**.
11. CEO and management vacancies must be filled within **3 months**.

#### 5. Employee Code of Conduct

1. Employees must sign formal conduct commitments.
2. Employees shall not engage in activities conflicting with institutional interests or conduct unauthorized personal business.
3. Part-time external work requires prior written management approval.
4. Active employees cannot serve as directors/advisors in other licensed entities (except subsidiaries or non-profit social entities).
5. Misuse of office, insider information, or unauthorized alteration of accounts carries severe legal penalties.
6. Minimum 3% of total annual staff salary expenses must be dedicated to employee training and career development. Unspent training funds must be carried forward into a dedicated Training Reserve.

#### 6. Internal Board Committees

The Board shall form the following mandatory specialized committees chaired by Non-Executive Directors:

1. **Audit Committee** (3 members; internal auditor acts as Member Secretary)
2. **Risk Management Committee** (3–5 members; CRO acts as Member Secretary)
3. **Staff Services & Facilities Committee**
4. **Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Committee**

#### 7. Credit Restrictions to Related Parties

Institutions are strictly prohibited from extending credit, funded/non-funded facilities, or accepting collateral from Directors, Executives, >0.5% Promoter Shareholders, >1% General Shareholders, Auditors, Legal Advisors, and their immediate family members/associated firms.

#### 8. Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR)

Institutions must allocate at least **1% of Net Profit** annually into a dedicated CSR Fund, spent strictly in alignment with NRB Guidelines on CSR.

#### 9. Repeal and Savings

Directive No. 6/081 and associated circulars issued up to Magh 2, 2082 B.S. are hereby repealed. Actions taken under repealed directives shall be deemed valid under this directive.

# 7. Provisions Relating to Implementation Schedule of Supervisory Directives

Exercising the powers conferred by Section 79 of the Nepal Rastra Bank Act, 2001, the following directives are issued to implement, and cause to implement, the instructions given during the inspection and supervision of "Class A", "Class B", and "Class C" licensed institutions:

Within a maximum of 30 days from the date of receiving the on-site inspection report, the Board of Directors of the licensed institution must rectify the irregularities mentioned and comply with the directives given. The institution shall submit its response, including the status of improvements on points raised in the report, to the relevant Supervision Department of this Bank. * **(b)** Details of classified and reclassified assets;


* **(c)** Estimated amount of retained earnings;


* **(d)** Additional resources arranged and time schedule set to fulfill future capital requirements as prescribed by the directives.



#### 5. Asset Liability Management (ALM)

The updated policies must be submitted to the relevant Supervision Department. Among other things, the amendments must pay attention to:

* **(a)** Cost and utilization of borrowed funds;


* **(b)** Amount of liquid assets needed to meet contingent requirements;


* **(c)** Short-term financial resource arrangements to handle potential deposit fluctuations;


* **(d)** Alignment between loan volume, off-balance-sheet investment commitments, and funding sources; and

# 8. Provisions Relating to Investments and Subsidiary Companies

Exercising the powers conferred by Section 79 of the Nepal Rastra Bank Act, 2001, the following directives are issued regarding investments by "Class A", "Class B", and "Class C" licensed institutions.

#### 1. Investment Policy and Procedures

Policies and procedures regarding investments in government securities, NRB bonds, and shares/debentures of corporate bodies must be approved by the Board of Directors before implementation.

#### 2. Investment in Government Securities and NRB Bonds

Licensed institutions face no restrictions on investing in government securities and NRB bonds.

#### 3. Investment in Shares, Debentures, and Other Financial Instruments of Corporate Bodies

* **(1)** Investments in shares and debentures of listed corporate bodies (listed on stock exchanges) can only be made for a duration exceeding 6 months. BFIs are prohibited from making short-term investments in corporate shares through any arrangement.


* **(2)** If an investment is made in shares or debentures of an unlisted corporate body, and it fails to list within 3 years from the investment date, an equivalent amount must be debited from retained earnings and credited to the Investment Adjustment Reserve. Funds in this reserve cannot be utilized until the securities are listed.


However, maintaining an Investment Adjustment Reserve is not mandatory for investments in:


* **(a)** Companies where licensed institutions hold 51% or more shareholding and are not required to be listed by existing laws;


* **(b)** Shares of Credit Information Bureau Ltd., Nepal Clearing House Ltd., National Banking Institute, Nepal Stock Exchange Ltd., and Infrastructure Development Bank;


* **(c)** Companies operating payment switches established jointly by BFIs to modernize Nepal's payment system infrastructure;


* **(d)** Debentures issued to raise funds for specific projects by entities established to invest in government-designated infrastructure sectors.




* **(3)** Investment in shares, debentures, or mutual funds of a single corporate body shall not exceed 10% of Core (Tier 1) Capital, and total investment across all corporate securities/mutual funds shall not exceed 30% of Core Capital. Furthermore, investment in a corporate body's shares and debentures cannot exceed 10% of that entity's paid-up capital. Any excess investment made beyond these limits at the time of investment must be deducted from Tier 1 Capital when calculating total Capital Adequacy.


* **(4)** Total investments in companies where the institution has financial interest shall not exceed 20% of Core Capital. The entire amount invested in such entities must be deducted from Tier 1 Capital during capital adequacy calculation.


* **(5)** Total investments made in Private Equity and Venture Capital equity shares are exempt from mandatory deduction from Tier 1 Capital during capital adequacy calculations.


* **(6)** BFIs are prohibited from investing in shares of "Class D" Microfinance Financial Institutions (MFIs), except for the purpose of counting towards Deprived Sector Lending as per Sub-clause (f) of Clause 4 in NRB Directive No. 17.


* **(7)** The Chairman/Board Members of the parent company cannot serve as Chairman/Board Members of the subsidiary company.



#### 4. Provisions Relating to Subsidiary Companies

Subject to these directives, licensed institutions may establish subsidiaries for the following purposes:

##### (1) Provisions on "Class D" Microfinance Subsidiaries

*(a)* BFIs may establish a separate "Class D" Microfinance Institution as a subsidiary to extend loans to the deprived sector, subject to the following policy and procedural arrangements:

1. The parent company must present consolidated financial statements. Accounting policies and statements must adhere to NRB Directive No. 4 guidelines. Subsidiary policies, procedures, and IT systems must align with the parent company's frameworks.


2. Capital adequacy must be calculated separately as well as on a consolidated basis. Consolidated capital adequacy must comply with standards prescribed for the respective class.


3. Transactions between parent and subsidiary must be conducted at Arm's Length Market Rates. These must be disclosed under "Related Party Transactions" due to inherent financial interest.


4. Details of transactions must be disclosed in regularly published financial statements. Non-compliance with arm's-length pricing or disclosure rules will invite fines and penalties under Sections 99 and 100 of the NRB Act, 2001.


5. Guidelines under Directive No. 14 regarding branch/office establishment apply to setting up subsidiaries:


* *(i)* Meeting minimum paid-up capital requirements.


* *(ii)* Meeting capital adequacy ratios set by NRB in the preceding month's financial report.


* *(iii)* Non-performing loans (NPLs) not exceeding 5% in the previous quarter.





*(b)* Promoter share investments and loans extended to "Class D" subsidiaries can count toward Deprived Sector Lending requirements.

*(c)* To encourage investment in the deprived sector, promoter shares in "Class D" subsidiaries are exempt from the requirement to deduct investments from Tier 1 Capital. Financial interest deductions for equity investments in such subsidiaries are also exempted.

##### (2) Merchant Banking Subsidiaries

BFIs may set up merchant banking subsidiaries (with at least 51% equity ownership) licensed by the Securities Board of Nepal (SEBON), under the following conditions:

* **(a)** The full amount invested must be deducted from Tier 1 Capital during capital adequacy calculations due to existing financial interest.


* **(b)** BFIs are exempted from the 10% paid-up capital cap when investing in shares/debentures of companies for merchant banking subsidiary setup.


* **(c)** Regulation and supervision fall under SEBON, but this does not restrict NRB from inspecting/supervising the subsidiary as needed.


* **(d)** Consolidated financial statements must be submitted in line with Directive No. 4.


* **(e)** Inter-company transactions must be disclosed under "Related Party Transactions". Violations are punishable under Sections 99 and 100 of the NRB Act.


* **(f)** Subsidiaries cannot:


* *(i)* Engage in securities brokerage business.


* *(ii)* Trade securities issued by themselves or their parent companies.




* **(g)** Parent companies cannot invest in securities issued by the subsidiary to raise funds, nor provide loans pledged against such securities.


* **(h)** Provisions regarding acquiring existing merchant banking companies:
1. Prior approval from NRB is required.


2. Submissions must include Board resolutions, MOUs, valuation reports of assets/liabilities, basis of valuation, and recent audited financial reports.





##### (3) Securities Brokerage/Dealer Subsidiaries

Commercial banks may set up subsidiaries (minimum 51% ownership) licensed by SEBON for securities business, subject to:

* **(a)** Investment deduction from Tier 1 Capital during capital adequacy calculation.


* **(b)** Exemption from the 10% paid-up capital limit for setup purposes.


* **(c)** SEBON supervision, while reserving NRB's supervisory authority.


* **(d)** Mandatory consolidation of financial statements.


* **(e)** Mandatory disclosure of Related Party Transactions, subject to statutory penalties.


* **(f)** Prohibition from trading securities issued by themselves or parent entities.


* **(g)** Parent company prohibited from buying debt instruments issued by subsidiaries or lending against them.


* **(h)** Subsidiaries are strictly prohibited from engaging in businesses outside securities operations.



##### (4) Managing Mutual Funds

Under Section 49(1)(ya) of the Bank and Financial Institutions Act (BAFIA), 2017, BFIs can operate mutual funds through subsidiaries, provided:

1. A fund manager subsidiary is set up, assigned risk weightings as specified by NRB.


2. The institution has completed 5 years of operations.


3. The institution has been profitable for the last 3 consecutive years without accumulated losses.


4. Net worth is positive.


5. Directors or CEO have not faced disciplinary actions from NRB (or 3 years have passed since such actions were cleared).



#### 5. Underwriting of Shares and Debentures

* **(1)** Class "A" institutions have no limit on underwriting securities. Class "B" and "C" institutions can underwrite up to 20% of Tier 1 Capital per company, and up to 100% of Tier 1 Capital across all companies combined. Limits are exempt when underwriting securities issued by NRB-licensed institutions.


* **(2)** If underwritten shares remain unsubscribed and are taken up by the BFI, they must be disposed of within 1 year. Failing this, the excess portion will be deducted from Tier 1 Capital during capital adequacy calculations.



#### 6. Investment Review

BFIs must review their investments bi-annually. The review must be verified by internal auditors to ensure compliance with policy and directives, approved by management, and filed within 1 month after the end of each half-year.

#### 7. Valuation of Shares and Debentures

Securities held by BFIs must be categorized per forms 8.1, 8.2, and 8.3 and valued in accordance with Directive No. 4.

#### 8. Purchase/Construction of Immovable Assets for Own Use

BFIs cannot purchase real estate except for operational needs. Land/buildings for self-use may be acquired or constructed under the following conditions:

* **(a)** All pre-operating expenses have been fully written off.


* **(b)** The first AGM following the public share issue (IPO) has taken place.


* **(c)** The institution has operated with net profit for the past 2 years without accumulated losses.


* **(d)** Capital adequacy matches NRB norms.


* **(e)** Total expenditure stays within 25% of Core (Tier 1) Capital.


* **(f)** Violations of items (a)–(d) require an equivalent deduction from Tier 1 Capital.


* **(g)** Expenses exceeding the 25% limit in (e) will be deducted from Tier 1 Capital.


* **(h)** Property not brought into self-use within 3 years of purchase/completion must be deducted from Tier 1 Capital.


* **(i)** Properties not intended for self-use must be sold.


* **(j)** Non-Banking Assets (NBA) capitalized for self-use are also subject to rules (a)–(i).



#### 9. Additional Provisions Relating to Investments

* **(1)** BFIs cannot invest in share capital, hybrid capital instruments, or unsecured subordinated debt issued by other "Class A", "Class B", or "Class C" BFIs.


* **(2)** Unless specified otherwise, "Core Capital" in investment rules refers to the Core Capital maintained in the preceding quarter.


* **(3)** Class "A" and national-level Class "B" institutions can engage in derivatives (Forwards, Options, Swaps, Futures) based on Board-approved guidelines. FX derivatives must follow foreign exchange laws and circulars issued by NRB's Foreign Exchange Management Department.

# 10. Provisions Relating to Transfer, Sale, or Ownership Change of Promoter Shares

Directives issued under Section 79 of the NRB Act, 2001 regarding promoter share transfers for Class "A", "B", and "C" institutions:

#### 1. Transfer Due to Death or Partition

In cases of death or legal partition among family members, promoter shares can be transferred to legal heirs/nominees following legal procedures, with notification sent to NRB within 15 days.

#### 2. General Sale/Transfer Rules for Promoter Shares

* **(a)** BFIs must seek prior approval from NRB (via Board recommendation) before transferring or selling promoter shares.


* **(b)** Transfers between existing promoters require 15-day prior/post notification. However, if buying shares exceeding 2% of total paid-up capital, prior NRB approval is required.


* **(c)** Except for death or legal partition, promoter shares must first be offered exclusively to existing promoters within the same promoter group.


* **(d)** Transfers to outside persons/entities are permitted only if existing promoters refuse to buy. External buyers automatically become part of the promoter group.


* **(e)** **Disqualifications:** Promoter shares cannot be sold/transferred to persons/entities who:


1. Have been removed from CIB blacklists for less than 3 years.


2. Are internal/external auditors of the institution.


3. Serve as legal or other advisors to the BFI.


4. Hold contractual stakes/partnerships with the institution.


5. Have unpaid tax dues or non-regularized PAN status.


6. Have been convicted by a court for dishonesty/fraud in BFI operations.


7. Are stockbrokers, market makers, or cooperatives.


8. Hold outstanding loans or financial interests with the BFI (Exempt for Class "D" MFIs).


9. Would cross the maximum threshold of 15% shareholding post-purchase.


10. Corporate entities that have not operated at net profit for the last 2 years.


11. Fail eligibility terms under BAFIA, 2017 Sections 11 and 12.


12. Have unpaid call money on issued shares.


13. Fail the rule that at least one-third of the post-transfer promoter group must hold a Bachelor’s degree or equivalent.


14. Do not meet NRB’s qualification and experience criteria.


15. Fail to submit prescribed applications or are disqualified by law.


16. Have criminal records where 5 years have not elapsed post-acquittal/sentence completion.




* **(f)** Corporate buyers must obtain NRB approval before transferring their own internal equity structure.


* **(g)** Buyers must submit updated source-of-income disclosures.


* **(h)** Promoter shares cannot be purchased using loans/borrowed funds from any BFI.



#### 3. Execution Timeline

Approved promoter share sales must be completed within 3 months of NRB approval, after which the approval automatically expires.

#### 4. Disclosures of Income Source

Promoter shareholders buying shares during capital increases must submit income source declarations using the prescribed format.

#### 5. Penalties for Illegal Transfer

If shares are sold in violation of these directives, the seller and buyer are barred for at least 5 years from acting as promoters or directors in any NRB-licensed BFI.

#### 6. Investment Limits & Cross-Holdings

* **(a)** Maximum promoter shareholding limits: Up to 15% in one BFI and up to 1% in other BFIs (Limits for Class "D" are 25% and 10% respectively). Limits do not apply to Government, NRB, or BFI investments in subsidiaries.


* **(b)** Promoters holding shares exceeding these limits are barred from receiving cash dividends, bonus shares, or participating in rights issues until compliance is restored.


* **(c)** Government-backed financial entities (EPF, CIT, National Insurance Corporation) holding excess shares prior to this directive must align within limits by end-Ashadh 2087.



#### 7–13. Operational Guidelines

* Cross-holding investments between BFIs are strictly prohibited.


* Cooperatives cannot invest in shares of NRB-licensed institutions.


* Shares issued via private placements or held by pre-IPO founders are regulated as promoter shares.


* Directors, executive officers, and family members are prohibited from subscribing to unallocated public issues unless the offer period lapses unsubscribed.



#### 14. Prior Approval Thresholds

Promoters owning 1% or more promoter shares in any BFI must seek NRB approval before buying/selling promoter shares in *any* other bank.

#### 15. Special Provisions on Share Transfers

* Direct sales between existing promoters or outside entities do not require NRB approval if the seller holds 2% or less paid-up capital.


* Subdividing share holdings above 2% to exploit this exemption is prohibited.


* If promoter holdings are auctioned or foreclosed due to loan default, sales must be conducted via sealed competitive bidding. Buyers holding 2% or more post-purchase must obtain NRB approval.



#### 16. Conversion of Promoter Shares (>51%) to Public Shares

BFIs operating for over 10 years with promoter holdings exceeding 51% may convert excess promoter shares into public shares on a pro-rata basis, down to the 51% minimum threshold.

* Conversion requires Board recommendation and NRB approval.


* Maximum conversion allowed per tranche is 10 percentage points.


* Converted public shares cannot be converted back to promoter status.



#### 17. Capital Expansion Regulations

* Public equity ratios must not be diluted during capital increases.


* Adding new promoters during capital increases is prohibited except via Right Renounce options under approved capital plans.


* Ultimate beneficial owners (natural persons) of corporate shareholders must be disclosed within 90 days of the fiscal year-end.



#### 18. Cross Holdings

BFIs are prohibited from cross-holding shares in each other.

#### 19. Provisions on Rights Shares & Calls in Advance

* Unsubscribed rights shares must be canceled automatically unless cancellation harms mandatory capital ratios, in which case they may be auctioned at a minimum base price of NPR 100 per share. Premiums collected must be credited to a separate Share Premium account.


* Advance capital collected for rights issues (`Calls in Advance`) can count toward capital adequacy and branch expansion requirements, but cannot earn interest, receive dividends, or be withdrawn/divested prior to formal capitalization.



#### 20. Foreign Investments

* Only foreign banks or financial institutions are eligible to invest as foreign promoters in licensed institutions under Section 5(1) of BAFIA, 2017.


* Sale or purchase of foreign equity stakes requires prior NRB approval.



#### 21. Unified Promoter Grouping

All promoter shareholders of a licensed BFI must be categorized under a single unified "Promoter Group". Articles of Association (MoA/AoA) must be amended accordingly at the next AGM.

#### 22. Repeal and Savings

1. NRB Directive No. 10/2081 and related circulars up to Magh 2, 2082 are repealed.


2. All acts performed under the repealed directives remain valid under these current directives.

# 11. Provisions Relating to Consortium Financing

In order to make loans disbursed under **Consortium Financing** by Class "A", "B", and "C" licensed institutions systematic and effective, Nepal Rastra Bank, exercising the powers conferred by Section 79 of the Nepal Rastra Bank Act, 2058, has issued the following directives:

#### 1. Definition of Consortium Financing

Unless the subject or context otherwise requires, "Consortium Financing" in this directive refers to loan, credit, and facility disbursed by two or more licensed institutions to a single client, firm, company, or project based on mutual understanding and agreement.

#### 2. Provisions Regarding Consortium Financing

Firms or companies utilizing loans disbursed under consortium financing shall conduct banking transactions exclusively with the Lead Bank and participating banks. This provision shall apply equally to Loose Consortium loans. However, once a consortium group is formed, this provision shall not prevent the admission of a new member with the consent of that group.

> **Note:** "Loose Consortium" refers to credit facilities/loans provided by two or more banks and financial institutions maintaining security via *pari passu* without forming a full consortium group.
> 
> 

#### 3. Prerequisites for Participation in Consortium Financing

1. Institutions licensed by this Bank to conduct financial transactions and the **Hydroelectricity Investment and Development Company Limited (HIDCL)** (only in relevant sectors) are eligible to participate in consortium financing. However, this provision shall not prevent the Employees Provident Fund (EPF) and Citizen Investment Trust (CIT) from participating and disbursing loans in a consortium group led by a NRB-licensed institution or HIDCL (in relevant sectors).


* **Clarification:** For the purpose of this directive, "Lead Institution" refers to any licensed institution selected as the lead by the consortium group.




2. If financial institutions other than those mentioned in the proviso clause of sub-bullet (1) are included in consortium financing, Nepal Rastra Bank must be informed.



#### 4. Restrictions on Other Institutions Disbursing Credit and Opening Client Accounts

1. A licensed institution that is not part of the consortium group shall not disburse any additional loans, advances, or facilities over the loan disbursed by the consortium group.


* *Exception:* In special circumstances, a non-participating licensed institution may disburse additional credit over the existing consortium loan upon obtaining approval from the consortium group.


* **Clarification:** "Consortium Group" refers to the group of institutions participating in consortium financing.




2. A client, firm, company, or project that has obtained credit/facilities under consortium financing shall not open an account for such loans/facilities in any licensed institution other than the participating members.


* *Exception:* If special circumstances require opening such an account in another licensed institution, it may be done with the approval of the consortium group.





#### 5. Timeline for Credit Decision

After an application for credit is submitted, the consortium group formed, and the Lead Institution selected, the Lead Institution and participating members must convey their decision regarding whether or not to disburse credit/facilities within **ninety (90) days**.

#### 6. Selection of Participating Licensed Institutions

The borrower and the licensed institution to which the application for loan/credit was submitted shall have the authority to select other members participating in the consortium. However, once formed, new members cannot be admitted without the consent of the group.

#### 7. Formation of Consortium Group

All licensed institutions selected pursuant to Clause 6 must form a Consortium Group prior to disbursing credit facilities. The internal working procedures of the group shall be determined by the group itself.

#### 8. Selection of Lead Institution

Members of the consortium group shall select one licensed institution from among themselves as the Lead Institution based on factors such as willingness to invest the highest loan share, long-standing acquaintance with the client, experience in consortium financing, and capacity to render professional advice. If necessary, a Co-lead institution may also be appointed with defined duties and responsibilities outlined in the consortium agreement.

#### 9. Loan Disbursement and Recovery via Lead Institution

Disbursement of credit facilities under consortium financing, as well as recovery of principal and interest, must be routed through the Lead Institution. In the case of partial recovery of principal installments and interest, the recovered amount shall be distributed *pro rata* based on investment proportions.

#### 10. Functions, Duties, and Powers of the Lead Institution

The functions, duties, and powers of the Lead Institution are as follows:

* **(a)** Act as the lead and agent for the participating consortium members.


* **(b)** Coordinate among participating members and handle necessary correspondence.


* **(c)** Call a meeting of the consortium group at least once every six months.


* **(d)** Call a meeting within 15 days if a member requests a meeting due to special reasons.


* **(e)** Conduct project analysis, prepare reports, and provide copies to all participating members.


* **(f)** Analyze requirements if a short-term loan is requested for the project.


* **(g)** Draft terms and conditions of the consortium agreement based on member consensus and provide a copy to each member.


* **(h)** Prepare necessary documents for disbursing loans and facilities.


* **(i)** Arrange for collateral mortgage/pledge and handle all activities related to securing investment, including insurance.


* **(j)** Safekeep all security-related documents and provide copies to all participating members.


* **(k)** Periodically collect necessary data/information from the borrower and distribute it to all members.


* **(l)** Conduct periodic joint site inspections with members, prepare inspection reports, and distribute copies to all members.


* **(m)** Periodically evaluate project performance and inform all members.


* **(n)** Make arrangements so that participating members maintain uniform loan loss provisions.


* **(o)** Send all necessary consortium disclosures to Nepal Rastra Bank, conduct required correspondence on behalf of the group, and notify members.


* **(p)** Initiate legal proceedings against defaulting borrowers after securing a decision in a consortium meeting.


* **(q)** Collect service fees as decided by the group out of the permissible fees specified under Directive No. 20 for performing its role.


* **(r)** Recover principal, interest for the delayed period, and agreed-upon penalties from a participating member if that member delays disbursing its committed share and the Lead Bank disburses it on their behalf within its single-borrower limit.


* **(s)** Recover expenses incurred during consortium arrangement or legal action against defaulting borrowers from all members in proportion to their loan investment if such costs cannot be recovered from the borrower.


* **(t)** Obtain necessary information from the Credit Information Bureau (CIB) before disbursing loans; credit must not be disbursed if the borrower is blacklisted or has overdue loans.


* **(u)** Provide credit facilities to the borrower in a timely manner as outlined in the consortium agreement.


* **(v)** Take other necessary actions pursuant to the decisions of the consortium group, subject to NRB directives.



#### 11. Majority Decision Binding

If consensus cannot be reached among participating members on any issue, decisions shall be made by majority vote, calculated on the basis of loan investment percentage. In the event of a tie, the Lead Institution shall have the casting vote.

#### 12. Obligations and Duties of Participating Members

The obligations and duties of participating members are as follows:

* **(a)** Provide a Letter of Consent (Power of Attorney) authorizing the Lead Institution to act on their behalf regarding consortium matters.


* **(b)** Provide full cooperation required for the Lead Institution to perform its role.


* **(c)** Actively participate in consortium meetings.


* **(d)** Comply with all terms and conditions of the consortium agreement once a formal decision to grant credit is made.


* **(e)** Resolve mutual disputes through consensus in the spirit of consortium financing.


* **(f)** Refrain from self-serving actions that negatively impact other members.


* **(g)** Inform the Lead Institution after approving their credit share and make funds available in a timely manner.


* **(h)** Maintain transparency and exchange information regarding credit and borrowers mutually.


* **(i)** Execute decisions made in consortium group meetings as quickly as possible.


* **(j)** Refrain from investing beyond specified limits, taking unilateral loan recovery actions, or classifying the loan differently.


* **(k)** Make credit facilities available to the borrower in a timely manner as specified in the agreement.



#### 13. Provisions Relating to Additional Credit

If an additional loan must be extended to a borrower under consortium financing, it must be done based on the following:

* **(a)** The consortium group alone may decide to extend additional loan/credit facilities after analyzing borrower requirements. Additional approved loans must be contributed by all members in proportion to their prior exposure.


* **(b)** If a member expresses inability to disburse its proportional share of additional credit after a decision under clause (a), remaining members (or any member with others' consent) may disburse the remaining portion, or a new member may be inducted with the borrower's and consortium group's consent.


* **(c)** If participating institutions cannot provide additional loans/facilities requested by the borrower, they must inform the borrower in writing.



#### 14. Conditions for Exiting Consortium Financing

1. A consortium member may exit the consortium group without causing any impediment to **Project Completion** under the following conditions only:


* **(a)** If another member agrees to take over the share of the exiting member, or


* **(b)** If the share is transferred to a new member with the consent of existing members.




2. Any member taking over the share of an exiting member must assume all prior liabilities. The exiting member may sell its share at a discount or premium.



#### 15. Appointment of Auditors or Consultants

The consortium group may appoint independent auditors or consultants to evaluate the financial condition of the borrower periodically. Expenses for such appointments shall be borne by each consortium member in proportion to their credit investment.

#### 16. Uniform SOL Group Name

For consortium and *pari passu* loans, all participating banks and financial institutions must use a uniform **SOL Group Name** as decided in the consortium meeting.

#### 17. Reporting Requirements

The Lead Institution must mandatorily submit quarterly consortium details (for periods ending Ashwin, Poush, Chaitra, and Ashad) per Directive Form No. 11.1 to NRB’s Bank and Financial Institution Regulation Department and the concerned Supervision Department within **30 days** from the end of each quarter.

#### 18. Repeal and Savings

1. The provisions relating to Consortium Financing under Directive No. 11/081 of Unified Directives 2081 and all subsequent circulars issued up to Magh 2, 2082 on this subject are hereby repealed.


2. Actions taken under the repealed directives/circulars shall be deemed to have been taken under this directive.

# 12. Provisions Relating to Credit Information and Blacklisting

To make credit analysis effective across Class "A", "B", and "C" licensed institutions, and to regulate and streamline the credit information system and blacklisting procedures for banking purposes, Nepal Rastra Bank has issued this directive pursuant to Section 79 of the NRB Act 2058, Regulation 7 of the NRB Credit Information Regulations 2059, and for the purposes of Section 88 of the NRB Act 2058.

#### 1. Provisions Regarding Credit Information Bureau

**Credit Information Center Ltd.** (Credit Information Bureau/CIB), incorporated and operating under the Companies Act 2063, is designated as the Credit Information Bureau for the purposes of Section 88 of the NRB Act 2058 and Regulation 3 of the NRB Credit Information Regulations 2059.

#### 2. Submission of Borrower Details to CIB

1. Licensed institutions must update credit details on CIB’s online portal in the prescribed format as follows:


* **(a)** All approved credit facilities within **7 days** of disbursement.


* **(b)** All credit facilities overdue by more than 90 days within **15 days** of the end of the month.




2. Subsequently, details must be sent quarterly within **15 days** of quarter-end until the loan is fully settled. Any subsequent changes (settlement, regularization, extension, or restructuring) must be updated with CIB within **15 days**.



#### 3. Mandatory Credit Information Reports

1. Licensed institutions must obtain a credit report from CIB prior to disbursing new loans, renewing existing loans, or restructuring/rescheduling loans.


* *Exceptions:* Loans backed by gold/silver, fixed deposit receipts, government securities, credit cards up to NPR 300,000, and digitally processed short-form loans up to NPR 500,000.




2. Once credit information is obtained from CIB, obtaining reports from other agencies is not mandatory.


3. Licensed institutions must pay the prescribed CIB service fee.


4. Before opening any account, licensed institutions must verify that the depositor is not blacklisted per CIB records. No fee shall be charged to the customer for this check.



#### 4. Blacklisting Recommendation Process

1. Licensed institutions must recommend borrowers with defaulted credit amounts of **NPR 1 million or more** for blacklisting. However, institutions are not barred from recommending non-paying borrowers for blacklisting regardless of loan amount.


2. Upon receiving recommendations, CIB shall verify identity documents and place the borrower on the blacklist within **5 days**. Removal from the blacklist shall follow existing policy guidelines.



#### 5. Classification of Defaulters

Borrowers failing to repay loans from licensed institutions shall be classified as follows:

##### (1) Willful Defaulters:

* **(a)** Borrowers unreachable for 1 year past due date, absconded, or those who have not initiated restructuring/rescheduling or committed to payment.


* **(b)** Borrowers who avoid or create obstacles to selling pledged collateral despite having sellable assets to clear dues.


* **(c)** Borrowers operating multiple businesses who do not utilize earnings/assets to repay credit.


* **(d)** Borrowers engaging in fund diversion, siphoning off business revenues, creating other assets, using short-term loans for long-term uses, or transferring resources to subsidiaries against agreements.


* **(e)** Borrowers operating controlled projects/businesses through third parties (workers, employees) to hide their involvement.


* **(f)** Borrowers refusing to provide additional security or personal guarantees upon request after default.


* **(g)** Borrowers intentionally providing false information regarding collateral or business.


* **(h)** Borrowers involved in misappropriating collateral.


* **(i)** Borrowers who colluded with financial institution staff, incited unlawful acts, or used undue pressure to evade repayment.


* **(j)** Investors who artificially inflate project costs (overcapitalization) through incorrect financial, technical, or market analysis.



##### (2) Non-willful Defaulters:

* **(a)** Borrowers with genuine intent to repay who defaulted due to force majeure/uncontrollable events (e.g., conflict, severe economic crisis, unfavorable external conditions) causing business losses.


* **(b)** Borrowers who remained in contact despite missing payment deadlines, completed restructuring/rescheduling, and committed to acceptable repayment plans.



#### 6. Restrictions on Blacklisted Borrowers

Licensed institutions shall not disburse new credit, renew facilities, grant additional facilities, release remaining loan installments, or accept guarantees from blacklisted individuals, firms, or companies.

* *Exceptions:*
* **(a)** Existing loan balances may be restructured/rescheduled within NRB guidelines. Non-defaulting group entities of blacklisted borrowers who maintain regular payments may be exempt from this restriction for up to 6 months (extendable by another 6 months by the Board of Directors).


* **(b)** For pre-existing credit/debit cards, institutions may honor transactions incurred within validity before blacklisting.


* **(c)** Family members of blacklisted individuals may be granted loans upon Board approval, subject to double the standard loan loss provision (capped at 100%).


* **(d)** Regularized loans may be renewed.





#### 7. Special Provisions for Valuation Experts

1. Institutions must execute formal agreements with empanelled property valuers defining roles, duties, and legal liabilities.


2. Valuers must assess collateral independently without conflict of interest, adhering to international standards.


3. If collateral auctioned by an institution fetches less than **two-thirds** of its valuation, or if a valuer misrepresents property locations/types, CIB shall blacklist the valuer upon the institution's recommendation.


4. If low auction proceeds are due to force majeure without valuer fault, the Board may decide not to blacklist the valuer.


5. If the primary borrower is removed from the blacklist, the associated valuer shall also be removed.


6. Institutions must verify CIB records before empanelling valuers.



#### 8. Recommendation for Passport Seizure

Institutions may recommend through NRB that the Government of Nepal withhold or seize passports of blacklisted defaulters. Once blacklisted individuals contact institutions and restructure/reschedule their loans satisfactorily, institutions shall recommend the return of seized passports.

#### 9. Conditions and Procedures for Blacklisting

1. Borrowers with total facilities of NPR 1 million or more must be blacklisted under any of the following conditions:


* **(a)** Principal or interest installment overdue by more than 1 year (even for a single loan account). Board may grant up to 3 months grace period upon justification.


* **(b)** Proven misuse/diversion of credit funds.


* **(c)** Proven misappropriation of pledged goods/collateral.


* **(d)** Borrower absconding or unreachable for 90 days.


* **(e)** Borrower declared bankrupt under prevailing laws.


* **(f)** Legal proceedings initiated in court by the institution.


* **(g)** Recovery case filed at the Debt Recovery Tribunal (DRT).


* **(h)** Non-fund based facilities converted to forced loans remaining unpaid for over 90 days. (For construction contractors, forced loans resulting from guarantee claims must be overdue by 1 year during FY 2081/82 to trigger blacklisting).




2. **Other Conditions:** Fraudulent document usage (fake checks, drafts, foreign currency, cards), financial crime convictions, write-offs over NPR 200,000, unpaid cash fines imposed by NRB on bank executives/directors, and dishonored checks.


* *Note:* Construction firms shall not be blacklisted solely due to check dishonor until mid-Mangsir 2081.




3. **Check Dishonor Blacklisting Process:**
* **Insufficient Funds:** Bearers must apply to the bank within 6 months of check dishonor certification. Banks must report to CIB within **5 working days**.


* **Incorrect Signatures / Stop Payment:** Banks must issue a 7-day notice to the drawer. If payment is not settled within 7 days, the drawer must be reported to CIB for blacklisting within 5 working days.




4. **Parties Subject to Blacklisting:** Primary borrowers, proprietors, partners, guarantors (after exhausting primary collateral and giving a 90-day notice), directors, and shareholders owning **15% or more** equity in private/public companies. Family members of blacklisted individuals are **not** automatically blacklisted.


5. **Exemptions from Blacklisting:**
* Force majeure conditions (floods, landslides, earthquakes) evaluated and approved by the Board.


* Sick industries receiving re-financing until the facility period expires.


* Land/business acquisition by the Government of Nepal.




6. **Removal from Blacklist:** CIB must process removal within **3 working days** upon receiving bank recommendations following full repayment, land/asset acceptance (non-banking assets), or legal settlement.

# 13. Provisions Relating to Cash Reserve Ratio (CRR) and Statutory Liquidity Ratio (SLR)

Exercising powers under Section 79 of the NRB Act 2058, Nepal Rastra Bank issues the following directives regarding CRR and liquid asset maintenance for Class "A", "B", and "C" licensed institutions:

#### 1. Cash Reserve Ratio (CRR) Provisions

1. Class "A", "B", and "C" licensed institutions must maintain **4 percent** of total deposit liabilities as mandatory cash reserves with Nepal Rastra Bank. Effective from 2082/02/18, a minimum of **90 percent** of this required CRR must be maintained on a daily basis.


2. Class "B" and "C" institutions in locations without an NRB office may open a current account with a nearby Class "A" bank for CRR calculation. Interest-bearing deposits do not qualify as CRR.


3. **Penalties for CRR Shortfall:**
* **First instance:** Penalty calculated at the prevailing Bank Rate on the shortfall amount.


* **Second instance:** Penalty calculated at **1.5 times** the prevailing Bank Rate.


* **Third and subsequent instances:** Penalty calculated at **double** the prevailing Bank Rate.


* Calculations are based on 14-day (fortnightly) average deposit cycles.





#### 2. Statutory Liquidity Ratio (SLR) Provisions

Effective from end-Poush 2079, institutions must maintain SLR against total domestic deposit liabilities as follows:

* **Class "A" Commercial Banks:** **12 percent**

* **Class "B" Development Banks:** **10 percent**

* **Class "C" Finance Companies:** **10 percent**


##### Eligible Liquid Assets:

* **(a)** Investment in Government of Nepal securities


* **(b)** Balances held with NRB for CRR purposes


* **(c)** Cash in vault


* **(d)** For Class "B" and "C" institutions, balances held in commercial banks for CRR


* **(e)** Demand/call balances held by provincial financial institutions in other banks


* **(f)** Deposits collected by NRB under open market operations


* **(g)** Investment in NPR bonds issued by international financial institutions

# 14. Provisions Relating to Opening Branches and Offices

Exercising powers under Section 79 of the NRB Act 2058, Nepal Rastra Bank issues the following directives regarding branch operations:

#### 1. Branch Establishment Guidelines

* Prior approval from NRB is required to open branches/extension counters in Metropolitan and Sub-Metropolitan areas.


* **Prerequisites for opening branches in licensed working areas:** Complete a feasibility study, maintain minimum capital adequacy ratios, ensure real-time networking with Central Office, and secure Board approval. Applications must be submitted within 15 days of Board approval, and branches opened within **6 months** of approval.

* During emergency public holidays declared by the Government of Nepal, essential banking operations (Cash, Check Clearing, RTGS) must remain operational from **10:00 AM to 1:30 PM**.



#### 4. Extension Counters & Mobile Banking Units

* Extension counters cannot engage in fund-based or non-fund-based credit disbursements.


* Mobile Banking Units are permitted across the country (except inside Valley Metros/Sub-Metros) to extend deposit/remittance facilities to underserved areas.


* Unauthorized branch openings will lead to immediate closure and a **2-year prohibition** on expanding new branches.

# 15. Provisions Regarding Interest Rates

In exercise of the powers conferred by Section 79 of the Nepal Rastra Bank Act, 2001, the following directives are issued regarding interest rates provided on deposits and collected on loans and advances by Class "A", "B", and "C" licensed institutions.

---

### **1. Interest Rate on Deposits**

Subject to prevailing laws and the following provisions, licensed institutions may determine their own deposit interest rates and calculation methods.

* **1. Change Frequency & Notification:** Deposit interest rates may be changed only on a monthly basis. Information regarding interest rates applicable to deposits (in local and foreign currencies) for the upcoming month must be published prior to the start of the Nepali month. When publishing interest rates, the annual percentage interest rate must be mandatorily disclosed to avoid confusing customers. However, if the bank rate or policy rates set by Nepal Rastra Bank change, interest rates may be changed and published immediately.


* **2. Interest Rate Fluctuation Cap:** When determining and publishing interest rates on deposits, institutions may adjust rates by a maximum of 10% based on the average of the maximum interest rates (excluding individual deposit accounts for social security allowances, fixed deposit accounts for endowments, and remittance-only deposit accounts) and the average of the minimum interest rates (excluding call deposits) published by all institutions in their respective class for the preceding month. If the gap between maximum and minimum rates exceeds the directive's limit, either the maximum or minimum rate must be maintained as is, and other rates must be adjusted accordingly.


* *Example:* If the average maximum interest rate published by all Class "A" banks in the preceding month was 8.5%, Class "A" banks can set a maximum interest rate of up to 9.35% (an increase of up to 10%).




* **3. Interest Rate Band:** The difference between the maximum and minimum interest rates offered on all local currency deposit accounts (excluding call deposits) must not exceed 5 percentage points. However, this provision shall not prevent banks and financial institutions (BFIs) from negotiating higher rates through mutual consent for personal accounts receiving social security allowances or fixed deposit accounts accepting endowment funds.


* **4. Savings Rate Variance:** The variance among interest rates offered on various types of savings accounts must not exceed 2 percentage points. Any increase or decrease in savings account interest rates, or the introduction of a new savings account type, must be adjusted across all savings accounts proportionally.


* **5. Remittance Deposit Premium:** A minimum premium of 1 percentage point must be added to interest rates offered to Nepalese workers abroad for remittance funds deposited into remittance savings accounts, fixed deposit accounts transferred from such accounts, or remittance savings/fixed deposit accounts opened by their family members. This interest rate is exempt from the interest rate spread calculation mentioned in sub-clauses 4 and 5.


* **6. Call Deposits:** Call deposits shall not yield interest higher than 50% of the minimum interest rate provided on savings deposits. Cheques shall not be issued against call deposit accounts.


* **7. Fixed Deposit Maturities:** Individual fixed deposits with a maturity of less than 3 months and institutional fixed deposits with a maturity of less than 6 months shall not be accepted. Furthermore, fixed deposits that allow early withdrawal at any time at the pre-determined interest rate shall not be allowed. Premature withdrawal/payment of institutional fixed deposits prior to 6 months is strictly prohibited.


* **8. Dormant Accounts:** Interest must continue to be credited to interest-bearing accounts even if they become dormant for any reason.



---

### **2. Base Rate**

Base rates must be calculated on a monthly basis in accordance with "Base Rate Determination Guidelines, 2012" (Annex 15.1) and submitted in the format specified in Directive Form No. 15.1 within 7 days from the end of each month. Additionally, monthly and quarterly average base rates must be published on the institution's official website.

---

### **3. Interest Rate on Loans**

Interest rates on loans and advances, interest calculation methods, penal interest calculation methods, service charges, and commission rates may be determined in compliance with prevailing laws and the following standards:

* **1. Base Rate Linkage:** Interest rates on loans and advances must be linked to the "Base Rate" calculated in accordance with Annex 15.1. Interest rates for rescheduled or restructured loans must also be determined using the base rate.


* **2. Penal Interest:** Loan agreements with borrowers must cap the annual penal interest rate at a maximum of 2 percentage points. Penal interest shall be charged only on the overdue principal installment amount for the delayed period, and charging compound interest on penal interest is strictly prohibited.


* *Relief Measure:* Enterprises/businesses using credit facilities up to NPR 50 million from BFIs that clear their principal and interest dues (payable up to mid-July 2022) by mid-October 2022 shall not be charged additional penalties or penal interest.




* **3. Monthly Rate Publication:** Premium rates must be determined based on loan type and maturity period, and interest rates on loans/advances must be published monthly prior to the start of the Nepali month.


* **4. Premium Rate Revisions:** The premium rate added to the base rate must be explicitly stated per loan category. Increases to published premium rates may only be made semi-annually. However, reductions in premium rates may be applied in shorter intervals.


* **5. Premium Structure Example:** Premium rates added to the base rate must be clearly documented in loan agreements.


$$\text{Loan Interest Rate} = \text{Base Rate} + 2\text{\% Points}$$



* **6. Minimum Lending Rate:** No loan shall be extended at an interest rate lower than the average base rate of the preceding three months. This rule does not apply to concessional loans mobilized through BFIs under programs run by the Federal/Provincial/Local Governments or donor agencies targeting designated sectors.


* **7. Variance Among Similar Loans:** Interest rate spreads between similar types of loans and advances must not exceed 2 percentage points.


* **8. Fixed and Adjustable Rates:** BFIs must publish both Adjustable Interest Rates and Fixed Interest Rates for all periodic/term loans.


* **9. Offer Letter Guidelines:** The offer letter provided to a borrower must clearly specify the premium rate added to the base rate. The applied premium rate cannot exceed the premium rate published at the time of loan application.


* **10. Lock-in on Premium Rates:** Once a premium rate is agreed upon in the offer letter and approved, BFIs cannot increase the premium rate or introduce schemes that automatically increase rates after initial discount periods. However, additional penalty rates can be imposed if the borrower misuses the loan for unauthorized purposes or damages collateral assets.


* **11. New Credit Facilities:** When extending credit limits or issuing new loans, newly published premium rates shall apply.


* **12. Interest Rate Spread Compliance:** Setting premium rates for loan interest rate determination must strictly adhere to Section 4 (Interest Rate Spread provisions) of this directive.


* **13. Automated Adjustments:** Changes to variable loan interest rates must be adjusted monthly, capped by the net change in the average base rate over the preceding three months.


* Variable interest rate systems linked to the base rate must be automated through the Core Banking System (CBS) to adjust rates according to changes in the three-month average base rate. Rate reductions or decisions to refrain from increasing rates must benefit all borrowers uniformly.




* **14. Concession for Deprived Sectors:** Concessional interest rates (at least 1 percentage point discount) must be provided for deprived sector loans extended to senior citizens, Dalits, freed Kamaiyas, Vadis, Haliyas, conflict victims, single women, and persons with disabilities as per Directive No. 17.


* **15. Personal Term Loans:**
* **(a)** Personal term loans with a maturity period of over 1 year must be issued at a Fixed Interest Rate for the entire term. However, variable rates may be maintained if explicitly requested by the customer in writing.


* **(b)** Applies to personal home loans, auto/vehicle/hire-purchase loans, and other installment-based personal term loans with a maturity exceeding 1 year.


* **(c)** For existing installment-based personal term loans issued prior to this directive, terms may be converted to fixed rates upon customer request.


* **(d)** Rate or schedule modifications under this clause shall not be classified as loan rescheduling or restructuring.


* **(e)** Fixed rates for new loans under sub-clause (a) shall be determined and published semi-annually.


* **(f)** Rates on fixed-rate loans may be reviewed after the first 7 years, and every 5 years thereafter, subject to written agreement with the borrower.




* **16. Loans Against Fixed Deposits:** When extending loans against fixed deposit receipts, BFIs must obtain written consent from customers at the time of account opening specifying the applicable premium added over the fixed deposit rate or the base rate.


* **17. Wholesale Microfinance Loans:** Wholesale loans extended to Microfinance Financial Institutions (MFIs) under deprived sector lending must capped at a maximum premium of 2 percentage points over the base rate.



---

### **4. Interest Rate Spread Provisions**

* **1.** The average monthly interest rate spread between deposit and loan rates must be calculated using the formula in Annex 15.2 and published monthly on the website.


* **2. Maximum Spread Caps:** Effective from March/April 2023, the maximum average interest rate spread must not exceed 4.2% for Class "A" Commercial Banks and 4.8% for Class "B" and "C" institutions. Effective from June/July 2023, this cap is lowered to 4.0% for Class "A" and 4.6% for Class "B" and "C".


* **3. Non-Compliance Actions:** Failure to maintain the required interest rate spread monthly will result in the following penal actions (exempting institutions declared problematic during their status and for 2 years post-resolution):


* **(a)** Suspension of branch expansion outside local levels lacking commercial bank branches.


* **(b)** Suspension of refinancing facilities (excluding earthquake relief loans).


* **(c)** Prohibition on cash dividend declarations and distributions (except for bonus share tax purposes).


* **(d)** If the spread limit is exceeded in any month, it must be brought within limits by the end of the quarter. Failure to do so will prompt penal action against the Board of Directors and CEO under the NRB Act, 2001.





---

### **5. Other Interest Rate Regulations**

* **1. Immediate Notifications:** Interest rate changes on loans/deposits must be communicated to customers immediately via SMS/Email.


* **2. Interest Crediting:** If loan interest is collected quarterly, interest on savings deposits must also be credited quarterly.


* **3. Daily Balance Calculation:** Interest on deposits must be computed on a daily balance basis.


* **4. Newspaper Publications:** Revisions to interest rates or minimum balance requirements must be published immediately in national daily newspapers and on websites.


* **5. Reporting Timeline:** Interest rate updates must be uploaded to the NRB Supervisory Information System (SIS) portal within 7 days of revision.


* **6. Historical Data:** Historical data covering the past 3 years of loan/deposit rates, base rates, and spreads must be maintained on the website.

* **1.** NRB Directive No. 15/081 regarding interest rates under Unified Directive 2081 and all related circulars issued up to January 16, 2026, are hereby repealed.


* **2.** Actions taken under repealed directives shall be deemed executed under this directive.

# 16. Provisions Regarding Financial Resource Mobilization

In exercise of the powers conferred by Section 79 of the Nepal Rastra Bank Act, 2001, the following directives are issued regarding financial resource collection by Class "A", "B", and "C" licensed institutions.

---

### **1. Financial Resource Mobilization Limits**

* Commercial banks (Class "A"), national-level development banks (Class "B"), and national-level finance companies (Class "C") face no general limits on deposit mobilization.


* **1.** Non-national level Class "B" institutions may collect resources up to a maximum of **20 times** their Core Capital.


* **2.** Non-national level Class "C" institutions may collect resources up to a maximum of **15 times** their Core Capital.


* **3.** Borrowings by licensed institutions shall not exceed **one-fourth (25%)** of their total deposit liabilities.


* **4.** Financial resource limits are monitored based on the Core Capital maintained in the preceding quarter.



---

### **2. Institutional Deposit Caps**

* **(a)** Institutional deposits from a single firm, company, or corporate body shall not exceed **10%** of the institution's total deposits.


* **(b)** Total institutional deposits from government entities, public limited companies, savings & credit cooperatives, and related funds shall not exceed **50%** of total deposits.


* **(c)** Domestic currency call deposits accepted by Class "A" banks shall not exceed **10%** of domestic deposit liabilities, while Class "B" and "C" institutions are capped at **15%**.



---

### **3. Deposit Collection & Account Regulations**

* **1. Internal Regulations:** Institutions must formulate and submit their deposit collection rules and procedures to the Bank and Financial Institution Regulation Department and the concerned Supervision Department.


* **2. Dormant Accounts:** Accounts with no transactions for **3 years** (savings accounts) or **1 year** (call/current accounts) must be marked as **Dormant**. Activation requires KYC updates without requiring additional documentation beyond the customer's application.


* **3. Digital Activation:** Dormant accounts can be reactivated through electronic KYC verification processes.


* **4. Account Closure & Unclaimed Deposits:** Zero-balance accounts inactive for **10 years** may be closed after issuing public notices. Details of accounts inactive for 10 years or unclaimed under the BFI Act, 2017 must be submitted to NRB annually. Unclaimed deposits after **20 years** must be transferred to the NRB Banking Development Fund.


* **5. Unclaimed Dividends:** Dividends unclaimed for **5 years** must be advertised in national newspapers. Unclaimed amounts post-notice must be transferred to an "Unclaimed Dividend Account".


* **6. Account Eligibility:** Savings accounts can only be opened for natural persons and non-profit organizations. Savings accounts opened for other entities must be closed immediately.



---

### **4. Borrowings**

* **1.** Within the resource mobilization limits in Clause 1, institutions may borrow from licensed BFIs, firms, and companies.


* **2. Foreign Borrowings:** Foreign currency loans/grants from foreign governments/international agencies require prior approval from NRB.



---

### **5. Issuance of Debentures and Debt Instruments**

* **1. Core Capital Threshold:** Institutions with cumulative losses not exceeding 5% of Core Capital (based on audited financial statements) may issue debentures/debt instruments up to **100% of Core Capital** with prior approval.


* **2. Capital Redemption Reserve (CRR):** Issuing institutions must build a Capital Redemption Reserve proportionally from annual profits. Exemption applies if debt instruments count towards capital funds or resource mobilization calculations.


* **4. Commercial Bank Mandate:** Commercial banks must mandatorily issue debentures equal to at least **25% of paid-up capital**.


* **5–7. Agricultural & Energy Bonds:** Qualified commercial banks may issue Agricultural and Energy Bonds. Funds raised must be fully deployed in respective sectors within **3 years** (30% in Year 1, 60% by Year 2, 100% by Year 3).



---

### **6. Subordinated Term Debt & Preference Shares**

Subordinated Term Debt and Redeemable Non-Convertible Preference Shares may be issued under the following key conditions:

* Unsecured issuance.


* Fixed maturity and clear dividend/interest rates.


* Subordinated payout priority (settled after depositors/creditors in liquidation).


* Must be listed on the Nepal Stock Exchange within 6 months of issuance.



---

### **7. Perpetual Non-Cumulative Preference Shares**

* Issued only to institutional investors without a fixed maturity.


* Fully paid-up, unsecured, and issued directly by the institution.


* Dividend distribution authority rests solely with the institution and must be paid exclusively out of current fiscal year profits.


* **Loss Absorption Trigger:** If Common Equity Tier 1 (CET1) falls below **5.125%** of Total Risk-Weighted Assets, these shares must be converted into promoter ordinary shares upon NRB approval.



---

### **8. Inter-Bank Deposit Restrictions**

* BFIs cannot open interest-bearing deposit accounts in other BFIs.


* *Exception:* Class "B" and "C" institutions may maintain non-interest call deposits with other institutions.



---

### **9. Penalties for Excess Mobilization**

If resource mobilization limits or borrowing limits are breached, daily fines equal to the prevailing bank rate on the excess amount will be levied, along with a personal fine of up to NPR 100,000 on the CEO.

# 17 Provisions Regarding Deprived Sector and Target Sector Lending

Class "A", "B", and "C" licensed institutions must allocate at least **5.0%** of their total outstanding loans and advances (including bills purchased and discounted) to the **Deprived Sector**.

* *Definition:* "Deprived Sector" refers to low-income individuals, socially backward women, Janajatis, Dalits, persons with disabilities, marginalized communities, small farmers, artisans, laborers, and landless families.

* **Foreign Employment Loans:** Up to NPR 300,000 per person (up to NPR 500,000 for women).


* **Agriculture & Livestock:** Up to NPR 400,000 per family for vegetable/livestock/herbal farming or cold storage certified deposits.


* **Technical Education:** Up to NPR 200,000 collateral-free loans for technical education.


* **Low-Cost Housing:** Up to NPR 700,000 collateralized housing loans targeting deprived individuals.

### **4. Penalties for Shortfalls**

# 18. Provisions Regarding Upgradation, Scope Expansion/Contraction, and Mergers & Acquisitions

Issued under Section 79 of the NRB Act, 2001 for Class "A", "B", and "C" institutions seeking institutional upgradation, scope adjustments, or mergers and acquisitions.

---

### **1. Upgradation Criteria (Class "C" to "B", or Class "B" to "A")**

To be eligible for institutional upgradation, BFIs must satisfy all the following prerequisite criteria:

* Completed at least **5 years** of licensed financial operations.


* Net profit recorded continuously for the last **5 consecutive fiscal years**.


* No cumulative losses maintained.


* Average Non-Performing Loan (NPL) ratio below **5%** over the past 5 years (and below 5% at application).


* Capital adequacy ratios, single borrower limits, credit-to-deposit (CD) ratios, and loan loss provisions strictly met for 5 consecutive years.


* Business size (total deposits + loans) equal to at least **15 times** the minimum paid-up capital of the current class.


* Minimum customer base: **25,000** for Class "B" to "A"; **10,000** for Class "C" to "B".



---

### **2. Mergers and Acquisitions (M&A) Incentives**

NRB may direct mandatory mergers for institutions failing capital compliance, suffering 5 consecutive years of loss, or under single-group ownership.

#### **Concessions for Commercial Banks Completing Mergers:**

BFIs completing integrated operations following M&A are granted special concessions for 1 year post-integration:

* **1 year extension** on meeting sector-specific lending targets.


* **0.5 percentage point reduction** in Cash Reserve Ratio (CRR) requirements.


* **1 percentage point reduction** in Statutory Liquidity Ratio (SLR) requirements.


* **5 percentage point increase** in individual institutional deposit caps.


* **6-month cooling-off period exemption** for directors and executive management joining other BFIs.


* **1 percentage point concession** on allowable interest rate spreads.


* **1 year grace period** to bring non-compliant CD ratios within mandatory caps.


* Branch closures or consolidations within a 1 km radius permitted without prior NRB approval.

# 19. Provisions Regarding Anti-Money Laundering and Combating the Financing of Terrorism (AML/CFT)

* **1. General CDD Requirements:**
* **(a)** Institutions must identify and verify customers in accordance with Section 7A of the Act and Rules 4 and 5 of the Rules.


* **(b)** Institutions may verify customers under Rule 3 of the Rules through digital access to Nepali Citizenship Certificates or National Identity Card records.


* **(c) Electronic/Digital Onboarding:**
* **i.** Accounts may be opened or business relationships established electronically based on policies ensuring identity, geolocation, and verification.


* **ii.** Electronic onboarding procedures must define lists of required digital documents, reliable digital channels for sourcing them, and regular verification methods.


* **iii.** Adequate safeguards must be adopted to prevent risks and misuse inherent to digital transactions.




* **(d) Mandatory National ID Requirement:** Effective January 14, 2025, BFIs must mandatorily open accounts based on the National Identity Card or National ID number after verifying details through electronic records.


* *Exceptions:* Nepalese citizens residing abroad opening accounts online from overseas may do so using alternative documents. However, their National ID details must be submitted and updated within 35 days of their return to Nepal. Citizens unable to care for themselves, persons with disabilities, or helpless individuals may also open and operate accounts using alternative documents.




* **(e) Government Disbursal Accounts:** For government payments (e.g., social security), accounts must be opened based on details provided by the relevant government entity. Full CDD/verification must be completed prior to the first withdrawal.




* **2. Ultimate Beneficial Ownership (UBO) in Legal Entities:** If a complex ownership structure exists, natural persons exercising ultimate control or holding beneficial ownership across each layer must be identified per Rule 6(1–3) of the Rules.


* **3. Occasional Transactions:** For walk-in/non-account customers conducting occasional transactions exceeding NPR 100,000 (or foreign currency equivalent) in a single day or across multiple transactions, identifying details and documents must be collected.


* **4. Third-Party Beneficial Owners:** If a natural person's transaction involves a non-family third party suspected to be the true beneficial owner, that person must also be identified.


* **5. Cash Deposits Over NPR 100,000:** If anyone other than the account holder deposits cash exceeding NPR 100,000, identification documents of the depositor and the explicit purpose of the cash deposit must be obtained.


* **6. Biometrics & Fingerprints:** Based on risk, institutions must obtain thumbprints or biometrics from:


* **(a)** Account holders and operators (for minors, thumbprints of the operator/guardian suffice).


* **(b)** Account operators of legal entities/arrangements.




* **7. Exempt Entities:** For accounts of the Government of Nepal, government offices/bodies, statutory bodies, state-owned enterprises, licensed BFIs, public insurance companies, UN agencies/specialized bodies, and foreign embassies, personal details of account operators alone are sufficient. Government officials operating government accounts may do so based on official employee IDs.


* **8. Required Documents:** Documents listed in **Annex 19.1** must be collected. Additional documentation may be required based on customer risk.


* **9. Local User Committees:** Accounts for local user committees may be opened based on registration with relevant government bodies or local government recommendations. Committee officials and operators shall be directly responsible for account transactions (**Annex 19.1-M**).


* **10. Frozen/Defrozen Lists:** Institutions must maintain updated lists of frozen/defrozen accounts issued by NRB or other authorities as a core CDD reference.


* **11. Mobile Number Ownership:** Institutions must implement mechanisms based on risk to verify whether the mobile number provided belongs to the customer.



---

### **3. Sanctions Screening & Designated Persons**

* **1. Screening Systems:** Automated screening systems must be established to continuously check customer profiles against lists of targeted individuals/groups published under Chapter 6B of the Act and Rule 69 of the Rules (Ministry of Home Affairs lists).


* **2. Freezing & Reporting:** If a match/asset is identified, accounts, lockers, or funds must be frozen immediately. Details must be reported within **3 days** to the NRB AML/CFT Supervision Division (using Annex 10 format) and copied to the Financial Information Unit (FIU).



---

### **4. Politically Exposed Persons (PEPs)**

* **1. PEP Detection Mechanisms:** Mechanisms must be established to identify PEPs during account opening, ongoing transactions, and profile updates; track changes in PEP status; identify family members and close associates; and assess risk levels according to official position and involvement.


* **2. Information Sources:** PEP status must be verified using customer declarations, public domain information, social media analysis, commercial databases, statutory records, and official government/agency lists updated annually.


* **3. PEP Categorization:** PEPs must be categorized into:


* **(a)** Domestic PEPs (incumbent)


* **(b)** Domestic PEPs (retired/former)


* **(c)** Foreign PEPs (neighboring/regional countries)


* **(d)** Foreign PEPs (other countries)


* **(e)** PEPs of international organizations




* **4. Record Retention:** PEP records must be maintained for at least **10 years** after demitting office.


* **5. Family & Associates:** Customer information and detection tools must be used to screen for family members and close associates of PEPs.


* **6. National ID Deadline:** National ID details for all PEP accounts must be updated.



---

### **5. Beneficial Ownership (BO)**

* **1. Monitoring Systems:** Institutions must establish mechanisms to identify BOs, conduct close monitoring of BO transactions, and perform integrated monitoring of all BO-related activities.


* **2. Identification Steps:** BO details must be sourced from company registry filings, self-declarations, public records, social media, statutory registers, commercial databases, and government bodies.


* **3. Retention:** BO records must be retained for **5 years** following the termination of the business relationship.



---

### **6. Enhanced Customer Due Diligence (ECDD)**

* **1. Mandatory ECDD Applicability:** ECDD applies to high-risk customers, including:


* **(a)** Customers executing systemically significant or abnormally suspicious digital transactions.


* **(b)** High Net-Worth Individuals (HNWIs) (thresholds/red flags defined internally by the BFI).


* **(c)** Customers suspected of involvement in money laundering or terrorist financing.


* **(d)** Customers from high-risk countries (corruption, tax evasion, FATF High-Risk/Monitored Jurisdictions, UN Sanctions Lists).


* **(e)** Cash-intensive businesses or users of new financial technologies/instruments.


* **(f)** Persons convicted by a court of law for offenses involving moral turpitude.




* **2. ECDD Measures:** BFIs must verify source of funds/wealth, verify transaction rationale, enforce strict transaction thresholds/monitoring, and utilize international ECDD intelligence tools.



---

### **7. Simplified Customer Due Diligence (SCDD)**

Institutions may apply SCDD for low-risk scenarios pursuant to Section 7F of the Act and Rule 9 of the Rules.

* **7A. "Let's Open a Bank Account" Campaign 2019:**
Simplified account forms may be used for natural persons with no existing bank accounts (self-declaration required). Identification can be established via Citizenship, Passport, Voter ID, National ID, Driving License, Government ID, or Local Level recommendation. Annual transaction limit is capped at **NPR 100,000** (full CDD required if exceeded). BFIs may deposit NPR 100 into the account upon opening.


* **7B. Remittance Savings Accounts:**
Simplified CDD applies to Nepalese foreign employment workers. Account holders must declare they hold no other remittance savings accounts. BFIs may seed NPR 100. Account credits are strictly restricted to foreign earnings/pension, interest, transfers from remittance fixed deposits, and government payments.



---

### **8. CDD Updates and Ongoing Monitoring**

* **1. Digital Accessibility:** CDD records must be stored digitally for real-time risk assessment and supervisory reporting.


* **2. Refresh Frequency:** CDD profiles must be updated:


* **(a)** Annually for high-risk customers.


* **(b)** Immediately upon transaction anomalies.


* **(c)** Immediately if CDD remains incomplete.


* **(d)** Immediately if doubts arise regarding the accuracy or adequacy of existing data.




* **3–4. Information Gathering:** Staff must report informal/formal insights to update CDD. Only changed or missing details should be requested during updates.


* **5–6. Priority Areas:** CDD updates must prioritize high-risk predicate offenses identified in National Risk Assessments (NRA) and Mutual Evaluation Reports (MER 2023), such as corruption, tax evasion, human trafficking, casinos/e-casinos, precious metal/gem dealers, real estate agents, and cooperatives.


* **7–8. Contact Tracking & Risk-Based Requests:** Maintain separate lists for unreachable customers. Audited financial statements for legal entities should only be re-requested based on assessed risk.



---

### **9. Institutional Risk Assessment**

* **1. Annual Risk Reporting:** Institutions must perform institutional AML/CFT risk assessments and submit reports for each fiscal year to the AML Supervision Division and FIU (`fiupolicy@nrb.org.np`) within the **first quarter** of the following fiscal year. Risk models must cover customers, products, geographies, and delivery channels.


* **2. Methodology Submission:** Assessment methodologies must be submitted to the AML Supervision Division prior to execution.


* **3. Classification:** Risks must be categorized as High, Medium, or Low.


* **4. Action Plan:** Risk assessment outcomes must drive policy updates and the annual AML/CFT action plan.


* **5. Quarterly Reporting:** Quarterly progress reports (**Annex 19.2**) must be submitted within **15 days** of quarter-end.


* **6. Audit Verification:** Internal and external auditors must audit automated AML systems to verify targeted financial sanctions, PEP screening, and high-risk monitoring controls.



---

### **10–11. Systems & Electronic Reporting (goAML)**

* **1. goAML Mandate:** Threshold Transaction Reports (TTR) and Suspicious Transaction/Activity Reports (STR/SAR) must be transmitted electronically through the **goAML Production Environment**.


* **2. Supervision:** NRB will conduct on-site/off-site inspections to verify goAML reporting integrity and compliance.



---

### **12–13. Wire Transfers and Correspondent Banking**

* **1. Wire Transfers:** Incomplete or suspicious transfers must be rejected/suspended and reported immediately.


* **2. Domestic Pass-Through:** Cross-border wires routed through local transfers must capture originator details. Repeated anomalies must be flagged to the FIU.


* **3. Correspondent Banking:** High-standard due diligence (Section 7M of the Act) must be applied to cross-border relationships. Relationships must be terminated if respondent banks fail to comply with AML/CFT standards.



---

### **14. Ongoing Due Diligence**

BFIs must continuously monitor transaction activity, including card usage abroad, to ensure alignment with customer profiles and declared business rationale. Suspicious card usage must be blocked and reported.

---

### **15. Threshold Transaction Reporting (TTR)**

Transactions exceeding defined thresholds must be reported via goAML within **15 days**:

* **1. Reportable Thresholds:**
* **(a)** Single/Series Cash Deposit or Withdrawal in a single day: **NPR 1,000,000 or more**.


* **(b)** Cross-Border Electronic/Wire Transfers in a single day: **NPR 1,000,000 or more**.


* **(c)** Foreign Currency Cash Exchange in a single day: **NPR 500,000 or more**.




* **2. Source of Funds:** Source of funds declarations are mandatory for cash deposits of NPR 1,000,000 or higher.


* **3. Exempt Entities from TTR:** Government transactions, statutory bodies, interbank transactions, clearing checks, tax/fee deposits to schools/hospitals/insurance companies, and pension disbursements are exempt from TTR (though STR/SAR rules still apply).


* **4. Categorization:** TTR filings must be split into `TTR-Cash`, `TTR-Cross Border`, and `TTR-FCY Exchange`.



---

### **16. Suspicious Transaction / Activity Reporting (STR/SAR)**

* **1–2. Reporting Rules:** STRs/SARs must be submitted immediately via goAML following internal evaluation.


* **3. Law Enforcement Inquiries:** If an investigating agency requests details on an account that involves suspicious activity, an STR/SAR must be filed with the FIU.


* **4. STR Categorization:** STRs must be categorized prior to submission:


* `STR-PEP` (Involving Politically Exposed Persons)


* `STR-TBML` (Trade-Based Money Laundering)


* `STR-High`, `STR-Medium`, `STR-Low` (Based on internal risk ratings)




* **5. Attempted Transactions (SAR):** Attempted suspicious transactions must be reported as a Suspicious Activity Report (SAR) via goAML within **3 days**.


* **6–8. Refusal & Tipping-Off:** If CDD cannot be completed, account opening must be refused or the relationship terminated (and an STR filed). Tipping-off the customer is strictly prohibited; if performing CDD risks alerting the suspect, the BFI must halt CDD and file an STR immediately.


* **9. Commercial Use of Personal Accounts:** Using personal savings accounts for corporate/commercial transactions must be flagged and reported as an STR.



---

### **17. Tipping-Off and Confidentiality**

Directors, officers, and employees are prohibited from disclosing AML/CFT reports, documents, or investigations to customers or unauthorized third parties. Unauthorized disclosures face severe penalties under Section 44A of the Act.

---

### **18–19. Internal Controls and Compliance Structure**

* **1. Board Oversight:** The Board of Directors must formulate and enforce comprehensive internal AML/CFT controls.


* **2–3. Designation of Compliance Officer:** A management-level Compliance Officer must be appointed. The officer acts as the institutional focal point, drafts policies, investigates internal red flags, and has unrestricted access to all organizational records.


* **4. Dedicated AML/CFT Unit:** BFIs must establish a dedicated AML/CFT unit headed by the Compliance Officer. The Compliance Officer must not be a close relative of regulatory officials or senior BFI management.


* **5. Disciplinary Actions:** Employees withholding requested AML data from the Compliance Officer face mandatory departmental disciplinary action.


* **6. AML/CFT Committee Reviews:** The internal AML/CFT Committee must submit quarterly compliance reports to the Board of Directors.


* **7. Capacity Building:** Annual AML/CFT training is mandatory for shareholders holding $\ge 2\%$ equity, Board members, executive management, and operational staff.



---

### **20. Record Retention**

All CDD records, transaction logs, account files, and reporting documents must be safely retained for at least **5 years** after transaction completion or account closure in both physical and digital formats.

---

# 20. Provisions Regarding Financial Customer Protection and Financial Literacy

In exercise of the powers conferred by Section 79 of the Nepal Rastra Bank Act, 2001, the following directives are issued to Class "A", "B", and "C" licensed institutions to inform the public about services provided, ensure transparent fee structures, and enhance financial literacy through BFIs:

---

### **1. Transparency Measures**

Licensed institutions must publicly disclose terms and conditions, fees, commissions, interest rates, penalty charges, and procedures for all financial products.

* **(a)** Concise brochures detailing all services, accounts, and financial products must be made freely available.


* **(b)** Clear information must be provided in simple language covering account rules, fee structures, banking workflows, account closure steps, interest calculation methods, prepayment charges, default penalties, late fees, card usage terms, and regulatory precautions.


* **(c)** Customers must be allowed to access their own transaction records free of charge.


* **(d)** Interest rates on deposits and loans must be displayed clearly on Internet/Mobile Banking apps.


* **(e) Core Banking Narrations:** CBS entry narrations must be clear and descriptive; **alpha-numeric codes are strictly prohibited**.


* **(f)** Fee and interest disclosures must be published on websites and submitted to NRB per Directive 15.



---

### **2–3. Plain Language & Disclosures**

* Information must be clear and non-misleading; deceptive advertising is strictly prohibited.


* Customer documentation must be provided in **Nepali** (English allowed for foreign/international contracts).


* **Information Officers:** Information Officers must be designated at head offices, provincial offices, and branch offices pursuant to the Right to Information Act, 2007. RTI disclosure tracking updates must be published quarterly.



---

### **4. Accessible Banking Services**

* **Priority Counters:** BFIs must designate priority service counters for senior citizens, persons with disabilities, and illiterate customers.


* **Disability Infrastructure:** In local levels with more than 5 BFI branches, at least **1 branch** and **1 ATM lounge** must be made fully barrier-free for persons with disabilities.


* **Digital Access:** Services like ATMs, mobile banking, and internet banking must be made accessible once customer self-capability is assessed.



---

### **5. Notification of Changes**

Any changes to contracted interest rates, fees, or service conditions must be communicated to customers in advance. Public announcements must be published in national daily newspapers (for national BFIs) or regional/district newspapers (for regional BFIs).

---

### **6. Prohibited Account & Service Charges**

* **Free Services:** BFIs **cannot charge any fees** for opening accounts, issuing checks (including Good-for-Payment checks), stop-payment requests, balance certifications, account operations, account closures, statement requests, remittance credits, or ABBS services.


* *Exception:* A minimal fee may be charged if a customer requests duplicate statements for the same period.




* **Reactivation & Minimum Balance:** No fees may be charged to reactivate dormant accounts. Fees cannot be deducted if account balances drop below required minimum limits. Minimum balances must continue earning applicable interest.


* **Cards:** Domestic currency debit/credit card renewal fees cannot be collected during the validity period after initial issuance charges.



---

### **7. Check Payments and Clearing**

* **100% Credit:** The full check value must be credited to the beneficiary's account; processing fees must be billed separately.


* **Check Bounce Charges:** If a check bounces due to insufficient funds, **no fee can be levied on the presenting payee**. The check issuer must be blacklisted via the Credit Information Bureau (CIB) per Directive 12.


* **Free Electronic Clearing:** Electronic check clearing for amounts **below NPR 200,000** must be provided completely free of charge to customers.



---

### **8. Loan Service Fees and Penalties**

BFIs may charge administrative service fees, prepayment fees, and commitment fees subject to strict regulatory caps:

#### **Maximum Loan Processing Fee Caps:**

* **Class "A" Commercial Banks:** Max **0.75%**

* **Class "B" Development Banks:** Max **1.00%**

* **Class "C" Finance Companies:** Max **1.25%**

* **Deprived Sector Loans to MFIs:** Max **0.50%**


#### **Prepayment and Loan Swap Fee Structure:**

* **Paid within 2 years of disbursement:** Max 100% of the approved processing fee.


* **Paid between 2 to 5 years:** Max 50% of the approved processing fee.


* **Paid after 5 years:** Max 20% of the approved processing fee.


* **Fixed Rate & Project Loans:** An additional **1.0%** fee may be levied for prepayments or swaps during the first 2 years.


* **Loans up to NPR 5,000,000:** Exempt from all prepayment charges.


* **Unilateral Rate Hike Exemption:** Prepayment fees cannot be charged if a customer prepays/swaps due to a unilateral rate hike or altered contract terms by the BFI.



> **Penalty for Excess Fees:** If a BFI charges fees above regulatory limits, it must refund the excess amount **plus an additional 10% penalty credit** directly into the customer's account.
> 
> 

---

### **9. Grievance Redressal**

* **Grievance Desks:** BFIs must establish a "Notice and Grievance Redressal Desk" headed by a Senior Manager or higher. Officer contact details (Name, Designation, Phone, Email) must be displayed on the homepage.


* **Online Redressal:** BFIs must maintain an online grievance portal on their website homepage and link to NRB's portal (`gunaso.nrb.org.np`).


* **Bi-annual Reporting:** Grievance resolution statistics (**Annex 20.1**) must be submitted to the NRB Financial Inclusion and Customer Protection Division semi-annually (mid-January and mid-July) and published in annual reports.



---

### **10–13. Financial Literacy & Data Privacy**

* **Literacy Initiatives:** BFIs must embed financial literacy programs into regular activities adhering to NRB Financial Literacy Guidelines, 2021.


* **Data Confidentiality:** Customer financial information, account ledgers, and trade details must be kept strictly confidential unless disclosure is required by law.


* **24x7 Call Centers:** Information regarding services must be accessible via 24x7 Call Centers or Audio Notice Boards free of charge.


* **Bilingual Website:** Website content must be published in **Nepali**.



---

### **14–16. Miscellaneous Customer Rights**

* **Term Loans (>1 Year):** No additional fees (except third-party expenses like mortgage registration/insurance) may be collected after loan disbursement.


* **Unsolicited Services:** Unapproved financial services are prohibited. Unauthorised fee deductions require a **100% refund plus a 10% penalty credit**.


* **Online Bank Guarantee Verification:** BFIs must provide online verification tools for issued Bank Guarantees on their web portal.



---

### **17. Repeal and Savings**

Directive No. 20/081 and associated circulars issued up to January 16, 2026, are hereby repealed. Actions taken under repealed directives shall remain valid.
